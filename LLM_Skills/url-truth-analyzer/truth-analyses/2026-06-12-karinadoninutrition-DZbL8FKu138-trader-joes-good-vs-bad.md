## Claim-by-claim assessment

### Claim 1: "Chlorine chicken is always a bad idea" — air-chilled is better
- **Classification:** Misleading / oversimplified.
- **Reality:** In the U.S., chicken is typically chilled either by immersion in chlorinated water baths (a few ppm of chlorine, well below EPA drinking-water limits) or by air chilling. The chlorine residue on the finished product is negligible and considered safe by FDA and USDA. The EU bans chlorine-washed chicken largely due to concerns that the wash compensates for poorer upstream hygiene, **not** because residual chlorine itself is toxic at those doses. Air-chilled chicken is often preferred for **texture and reduced water uptake** (you pay for less added water), not because chlorine-rinsed chicken is dangerous to consumers.
- **SORT grade: C** (consensus-based, surrogate outcome — texture/water content, not health outcome).

### Claim 2: "Farm-raised salmon is one of the most terrible things you can do for yourself"
- **Classification:** Overstated.
- **Reality:** Farm-raised Atlantic salmon historically had higher levels of PCBs, dioxins, and lower omega-3:omega-6 ratios than wild salmon (Hites et al., *Science* 2004). However, modern aquaculture has substantially reduced contaminant levels through cleaner feed, and farmed salmon still delivers EPA/DHA omega-3s at levels associated with cardiovascular benefit. Major bodies (American Heart Association, Dietary Guidelines for Americans) recommend 2 servings/week of fatty fish **including farmed salmon**. Wild salmon is preferable on contaminant grounds for high-frequency consumers, but "one of the most terrible things you can do for yourself" is not supported.
- **SORT grade: B** (contaminant data is patient-oriented but the magnitude of risk vs. benefit favors continued consumption).

### Claim 3 & 4: Unspecified product pairs ("terrible ingredients," "anti-caking agent")
- **Classification:** Unverifiable from transcript; thumbnail does not show specific products. Anti-caking agents (e.g., silicon dioxide, calcium silicate, sodium aluminosilicate) are FDA-recognized as safe (GRAS) at typical food levels. Blanket "avoid" guidance is not evidence-based for the general population.
- **SORT grade: C** (expert opinion only; no consistent patient-oriented harm signal at dietary doses).

The **absolute language** ("terrible," "always," "avoid") overstates the evidence. Air-chilled chicken and wild salmon are reasonable preferences, not health imperatives. Anti-caking agents at GRAS levels are not a meaningful health risk. **SORT C overall.**
