## EBM SORT validation

### Claim 1 — Blueberries contain anthocyanins and fiber

**Verdict:** Accurate (compositional fact, not a clinical claim).
Blueberries are among the highest dietary sources of anthocyanins (~120–160 mg per 100 g for highbush; higher for wild lowbush) and provide ~2.4 g fiber per 100 g (USDA FoodData Central). No SORT grade needed — this is a food-composition fact, not a therapeutic claim.

### Claim 2 — Blueberry oats as a daily habit "helps you feel your best"

**Verdict:** Plausible, low-stakes general-wellness framing.
Whole oats (β-glucan ~3 g/day) modestly lower LDL cholesterol — an FDA-authorized health claim (Grade **A** for LDL reduction; Whitehead 2014 meta-analysis, PMID: 25411276). Habitual blueberry intake (≥1 cup/day for 6 months) improved endothelial function and lowered systolic BP in MIND-derived cohorts and small RCTs (Grade **B**). Calling the combo a habit that "helps you feel your best" is **not falsifiable** and is fine as lifestyle framing.

### Claim 3 — "Age Mates daily longevity blend supports healthy aging and daily vitality"

**Verdict:** Unsupported as stated. **SORT Grade C** at best (mechanistic / single-ingredient surrogate-marker data).
- "Age Mates" is a commercial multi-ingredient supplement marketed in the longevity space; common ingredients in this category include NMN, NAD precursors, resveratrol, spermidine, fisetin, urolithin A, CoQ10, and assorted polyphenols. **No product called "Age Mates" has a published RCT in humans demonstrating an effect on lifespan, healthspan biomarkers, or all-cause mortality.** This is true of effectively every consumer "longevity blend" on the market as of 2026.
- The most-studied individual longevity-adjacent compounds (NMN, NR, resveratrol, spermidine, urolithin A) have:
  - Robust **animal data** (mice, C. elegans) showing effects on NAD⁺, mitochondrial function, autophagy.
  - **Small short-duration human RCTs** (typically 4–12 weeks, n<100) showing changes in **surrogate biomarkers** (NAD⁺ levels, walking distance, muscle strength) — DOE (disease-oriented evidence), not POEM.
  - **No** RCT in humans powered for or demonstrating reduced mortality, reduced incidence of age-related disease, or extended lifespan.
- "Supports healthy aging and daily vitality" is **structure/function language** — the FDA/TGA-permitted vagueness that lets supplements market without proving disease prevention. It is not the same as evidence that the product makes you live longer or measurably healthier.
- **SORT C** for the broad category; **insufficient evidence** for the specific branded blend.

### Claim 4 — These daily habits increase chances of living longer

**Verdict:** Partially accurate, partially overreach.
- The **dietary-pattern** layer (whole oats + berries within a plant-rich diet) is associated with reduced all-cause mortality in large prospective cohorts (Schwingshackl 2017 umbrella review, PMID: 28792941; Aune 2017 fruit/veg meta-analysis, PMID: 28338764). Grade **B** (cohort-derived) for the specific oats+berries habit.
- The **supplement** layer adds no demonstrated longevity benefit and may add risk (cost, interactions, unregulated dosing). Grade **C / insufficient**.
- Bundling the supplement into the longevity claim is the core problem: the food habit does some of what is being promised; the supplement does not (so far as published human evidence shows).

## Bottom line

The food half of this reel — **blueberries (anthocyanins, fiber) blended into oats as a daily breakfast habit** — is accurate and supported by reasonable evidence (Grade A for oat β-glucan/LDL, Grade B for habitual berry intake on vascular markers, Grade B for plant-rich pattern and all-cause mortality).

The **supplement half — "Age Mates daily longevity blend supports healthy aging and daily vitality"** — is **promotional structure/function language with no human RCT support for the branded product and only Grade C mechanistic / surrogate-marker evidence for the ingredient class**. The product placement should be read as **advertising**; the absence of a visible `#ad`/Paid Partnership disclosure in the captured inputs is itself a transparency concern.

**What to take from the reel:** Eat the oats and blueberries. Skip the supplement unless and until a properly powered human trial of that specific product (or its ingredient stack) shows a meaningful, durable effect on a patient-relevant outcome.
