## Analysis

### Medical: SORT Analysis
The marquee claims rest on real landmark studies. The hippocampus claim (C2) maps to Erickson et al. 2011 (PNAS): a year of moderate walking grew the hippocampus ~2%, effectively reversing 1–2 years of age-related shrinkage, with BDNF as mediator. The depression claim (C3) is supported by a BMJ exercise-vs-depression meta-analysis and the **SMILE** RCT (Blumenthal), where supervised aerobic exercise matched sertraline for major depressive disorder — a strong, frequently-cited result, appropriately framed as "an option," not a blanket replacement. The metabolic claims are accurate: contracting muscle takes up glucose largely insulin-independently; walking lowers HbA1c (~0.5% in a meta-analysis of 18 RCTs in T2D); post-meal short walks beat one long walk for glucose AUC; step dose-response (8,800 vs 2,000 steps → ~60% lower mortality, with the steepest gain at the low end). The mitochondrial-biogenesis/mitophagy mechanism is sound. The "why some people don't benefit = inconsistency" framing is reasonable, if more heuristic than hard-evidenced.
