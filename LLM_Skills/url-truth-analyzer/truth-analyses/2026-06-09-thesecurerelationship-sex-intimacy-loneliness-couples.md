## Analysis

### Science: Claim Validation
The core links are supported. Sexual intimacy is associated with greater relationship and personal well-being, though the association is non-linear — Muise et al. (2016) found well-being rises with frequency but plateaus around once weekly, and "more" is not always better [1]. The "afterglow" mechanism (sex promoting lingering closeness/bonding) is documented by Meltzer et al. (2017), who found elevated sexual satisfaction persisting ~48 hours and predicting relationship quality [2]. The post's causes of low desire (stress, pain, hormonal change, emotional disconnection) are standard in sexual-health literature, and the explicit anti-pressure / "nobody is entitled to sex" framing is consent-positive and clinically responsible. No overclaiming (it never promises sex fixes a relationship).

## Verdict
The strongest claim is C3 (no entitlement to sex; pressure is never the answer) — it reflects clear consent ethics and avoids the coercive framing that plagues much "low-desire" content. The weakest is C2 (missing intimacy → loneliness), graded Moderate because the frequency–well-being link is correlational and non-linear, so absence-causes-loneliness is plausible but not strictly causal. Overall the post is accurate, consent-positive, and constructively actionable. Recommend "Yes." ## ELI5 — Friend to Friend
For lots of couples, sex is a big way they feel close — so when it dries up, one or both can feel lonely, and that's normal, not shameful. The healthy move is to get curious together about what's getting in the way, never to pressure anyone. The science agrees, and notably says more sex isn't automatically better (about weekly is where well-being levels off). If this is you, try one low-stakes conversation about what closeness means to each of you.
