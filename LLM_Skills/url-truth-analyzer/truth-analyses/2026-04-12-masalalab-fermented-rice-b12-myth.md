# Truth Analysis: Fermented Rice Does Not Provide Vitamin B12
**Source URL**: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DW9T51OieXv/
**Analyzed**: 2026-04-12
**Content type**: Medical
**Format**: Video (Instagram Reel, Hindi)

**Share?**: Yes — this is accurate, responsible myth-busting from a credible science communicator. Krish Ashok correctly debunks the fermented rice = B12 claim and properly advocates for testing and supplementation.

## Summary
Krish Ashok (Masala Lab) debunks the viral claim that soaking/fermenting rice in clay pots increases Vitamin B12 levels. He explains that B12 is produced by soil bacteria, not the lactobacillus involved in rice fermentation. He critiques anecdotal "testimonials" (screenshots, comments) as non-evidence, describes what a proper RCT would look like, and recommends that vegetarians get tested and supplement if deficient rather than relying on unproven home remedies.

## Analysis

### SORT Analysis

#### Safety Lens
The reel promotes a safe approach: get B12 tested, take supplements if deficient. It explicitly warns against relying on unproven fermented rice recipes in place of evidence-based supplementation. No harmful advice is given. The implicit risk being addressed (B12 deficiency going untreated due to false belief in home remedies) is a real public health concern in India where up to 80% of vegetarians may be deficient.

#### Outcomes Lens
**Patient-oriented framing**: B12 deficiency consequences (memory problems, neurological damage) are referenced as patient-oriented outcomes. The advice (test + supplement) directly addresses preventing these outcomes.

**The debunked claim** is DOE-level at best: the viral recipe claims to raise B12 blood levels (a lab value / DOE), but even this DOE claim is unsupported.

#### Risk of Bias Lens
Krish Ashok is a well-known Indian science communicator and author of "Masala Lab." He has no disclosed supplement or pharmaceutical affiliations. His approach in this reel is methodologically sound:
- He identifies that anecdotal testimonials (comments, screenshots) are not evidence
- He correctly describes what a proper clinical trial would require (200 participants, control group, pre/post B12 testing, publication)
- His biochemistry claim (lactobacillus doesn't produce B12; soil bacteria do) is largely correct, with one nuance: some specific Lactobacillus strains isolated from human feces can produce B12 in lab conditions, but standard food fermentation lactobacilli do not produce clinically meaningful amounts

#### Total Evidence Lens
- B12 deficiency prevalence in Indian vegetarians (~70-80%) is well-documented in multiple studies
- Fermented rice/idli/dosa are NOT recognized as B12 sources in any clinical guideline (Indian or international)
- Some research shows certain specialized bacterial strains can produce B12 during grain fermentation, but these are not the strains present in traditional rice fermentation
- Standard dairy products contain some B12 but typically insufficient amounts for those already deficient
- B12 supplementation for vegetarians is recommended by WHO, Indian Council of Medical Research, and major nutrition bodies

### SORT Grade: **A** — Consistent, good-quality patient-oriented evidence

Rationale: The claim being debunked (fermented rice provides B12) has no supporting clinical evidence. The recommended intervention (testing + supplementation for vegetarian B12 deficiency) is supported by consistent evidence from multiple systematic reviews and clinical guidelines. The prevalence of B12 deficiency in Indian vegetarians is established across numerous epidemiological studies.
