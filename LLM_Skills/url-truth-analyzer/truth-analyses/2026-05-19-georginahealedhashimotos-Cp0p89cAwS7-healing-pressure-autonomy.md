## Analysis

### Medical: SORT Analysis

**Pass 1 — Four lenses**

- **Safety**: The post's *literal* spoken claim — "you can decline an intervention that conflicts with your priorities; resistance is not by definition a blockage" — is safe and ethically standard. The *contextual* risk is that this framing is delivered by a creator whose broader content discourages levothyroxine; viewers may generalize "no thank you, not my priority" to declining hormone replacement in overt hypothyroidism, which is unsafe (untreated overt hypothyroidism risks myxedema, dyslipidemia, infertility, miscarriage, preterm delivery, and impaired offspring neurocognition).
- **Outcomes (POEM vs DOE)**: No outcome is asserted. There is no surrogate marker, no patient-oriented endpoint, no comparative claim. This is meta-content about how to make decisions during healing, not a claim about what heals.
- **Risk of Bias**: High at the channel level (single-N self-report, paid programs, financial COI), but the per-post claim is not the kind that bias distorts in the usual way — it is an opinion/values statement.
- **Total Evidence**: The underlying principle (patient autonomy, informed refusal, shared decision-making) is supported by **A-level** consensus across medical ethics — AMA Code of Medical Ethics 1.1.3 (Patient Rights), WHO patient-rights framework, Beauchamp & Childress (foundational autonomy principle). However, this principle in mainstream ethics is paired with truthful disclosure of consequences of refusal — which Szabo does not provide.

**Pass 2 — SORT grade**

- **For the spoken claim "patient autonomy in declining interventions is valid"**: **Grade A** — this is established bioethics consensus, not a contested medical claim. There is nothing wrong with this statement on its own.
- **For the implicit channel-level claim "declining standard endocrine care for Hashimoto's is a reasonable exercise of that autonomy"**: **Grade C** — replacing levothyroxine with diet/supplements in overt hypothyroidism is not supported by current endocrine-society guidelines (ATA, Endocrine Society, NICE).
- **For per-post claim extractability**: **None extractable as a falsifiable medical claim**. Per the spec's hard rule, I am flagging this rather than fabricating a SORT analysis of a claim the post does not make.

## Verdict
Considered in isolation, the spoken content of this 63-second video is the least objectionable thing on the channel: it tells viewers that they have the right to say no to interventions that don't match their priorities, and that being pressured with rhetoric like "your resistance is a blockage you have to push through" is itself a red flag worth refusing. That is a genuinely sound principle and is consistent with Grade A bioethics consensus on informed consent and shared decision-making. The honest problem is the wrapper: a "HASHIMOTO'S HEALING TIP" overlay delivered by a non-credentialed creator whose monetized brand is "you can replace levothyroxine," where the autonomy frame functionally licenses viewers to decline the very treatment the evidence base supports for overt hypothyroidism. Share with the explicit caveat that "you can decline interventions" is true *and* must be paired with truthful disclosure of consequences, which this channel does not provide — otherwise it converts a legitimate autonomy principle into cover for refusing evidence-based endocrine care.
