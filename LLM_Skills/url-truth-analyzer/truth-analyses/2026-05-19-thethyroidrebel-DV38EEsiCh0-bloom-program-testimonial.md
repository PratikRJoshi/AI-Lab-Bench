## Analysis

### Medical: SORT Analysis

#### Extracted claims
1. **(Implicit)** The Bloom program causes meaningful recovery from thyroid dysfunction in patients who were previously unwell.
2. **(Implicit)** Anna's improvement since 2017 is attributable to "courses like Bloom."
3. **(Explicit, framing)** Bloom is "the best investment you can make" for women's health.

All three are testimonial / anecdotal claims with no numerical outcome, no comparator, no biomarker, no follow-up duration, and no independent verification.

#### Pass 1: Four lenses

**1. Safety lens**
- No safety information offered. The post omits any mention of: continued thyroid hormone monitoring, the danger of self-adjusting levothyroxine dose, drug-supplement interactions, or contraindications.
- The implicit promise of "healing" in autoimmune thyroid disease is a known vector for delayed presentation to endocrinology, particularly in Hashimoto's where untreated hypothyroidism carries cardiovascular and obstetric risks.

**2. Outcomes lens**
- No outcome is operationalized. "Feeling better" is a subjective POEM-shaped wrapper around no measured outcome.
- No POEMs (mortality, antibody titers correlating with morbidity, pregnancy outcomes, hospitalization). No DOEs (TSH, free T4, free T3, TPO/Tg antibodies).
- The single "data point" is one client's self-report.

**3. Risk of bias lens**
- Testimonial from a person commercially aligned with the product owner (selection bias + reporting bias at maximum).
- No control group, no comparator, no blinding, no pre-registration, no independent assessment.
- This evidence sits at tier 7 of the SORT bias hierarchy (anecdote / expert promotion).

**4. Total evidence lens**
- The Bloom program has zero peer-reviewed publications, zero registered trials on ClinicalTrials.gov, and no independent evaluation.
- The broader literature on multimodal nutrition/lifestyle interventions for Hashimoto's is thin: a small uncontrolled pilot of the autoimmune protocol (AIP) diet showed symptom-score improvement but no antibody change (Abbott 2019); selenium for Hashimoto's has a Cochrane review concluding insufficient evidence for patient-relevant outcomes; gluten-free diet in Hashimoto's has small RCTs with mixed signals on antibody titers but no demonstrated effect on clinical hypothyroidism.
- Standard of care for Hashimoto's hypothyroidism remains levothyroxine replacement titrated to TSH, per ATA and NICE guidelines.

#### Pass 2: SORT Grade Assignment

**Grade C** — Single anecdotal testimonial from a commercially affiliated client. No RCT, no systematic review, no patient-oriented evidence beyond one self-report. Even the underlying multimodal-program category that Bloom belongs to does not exceed Grade C in the published literature.

## Verdict

**Grade C — Do not act on this content as health guidance.** The post is a 60-second marketing testimonial for a £-priced 16-week alternative-medicine program from a BANT-registered nutritional therapist (not a doctor or registered dietitian). No clinical claim is made with sufficient specificity to be falsified, but the implied promise — that paying for "courses like Bloom" can give a thyroid patient "her life back" — has no support in peer-reviewed literature. Women with Hashimoto's or hypothyroidism considering this program should (a) maintain endocrinology follow-up and levothyroxine therapy, (b) never adjust their dose based on a program like this, and (c) recognize that "Anna's" testimonial is structurally identical to every other paid-program testimonial: one self-reporting client, commercially aligned, with no comparator.

## ELI5 — Friend to Friend

This isn't health information — it's a sales ad dressed up as a friendly chat.
