#### Overall Assessment
The post makes **2 legitimate points**:
1. TSH alone is insufficient for many Hashimoto's patients — adding FT4 and antibodies is standard of care.
2. Iron and vitamin D status affect thyroid function — checking them is reasonable.

And **4 questionable claims**:
1. FT3 as routine monitoring (not guideline-supported)
2. "Optimal" ranges narrower than clinical evidence supports
3. 24-hour cortisol rhythm as routine Hashimoto's testing
4. Reverse T3 as clinically useful (explicitly contradicted by ATA guidelines)

#### Closing claim: "Labs show patterns. They don't tell you the root cause."
This is a philosophical framing designed to redirect from lab interpretation (endocrinology scope) to "root cause investigation" (her coaching scope). It's technically true (labs are data, not diagnosis) but misleadingly implies that conventional lab interpretation is inadequate.

### SORT Grade: B/C (split)
- Comprehensive panel recommendation: **Grade B** (supported by guidelines for initial workup, though serial antibody monitoring is debated)
- "Optimal ranges" and rT3/cortisol testing: **Grade C** (functional medicine consensus not supported by RCT evidence)

## Verdict
The strongest post in this batch, containing genuinely useful information (get a full thyroid panel, check iron/D) alongside over-reaching claims (reverse T3, cortisol rhythm, narrower "optimal" ranges). A viewer who takes "ask for TSH + FT4 + TPO antibodies" to their doctor would benefit. A viewer who insists on functional medicine "optimal ranges" or reverse T3 testing would be pursuing low-value care. The danger is that the legitimate points lend credibility to the questionable ones.

## ELI5 — Friend to Friend
Actually partly useful! She's right that many doctors only check TSH when they should check the full panel (TSH + FT4 + TPO antibodies at minimum). Ask your endo for those. But ignore the "reverse T3" and "24-hour cortisol rhythm" stuff — even the American Thyroid Association says those are useless for routine management. And her "optimal ranges" are tighter than what evidence actually supports. Take the "get more labs" message, skip the functional medicine upsell.
