### SORT Analysis (Medical)

**Extracted claim** (from the thumbnail overlay, since there is no transcript):
> Castor oil packs help reverse Hashimoto's thyroiditis.

This decomposes into two sub-claims:
- **Sub-claim 1**: Castor oil packs have a clinically meaningful effect on Hashimoto's autoimmunity (anti-TPO/Tg antibodies, TSH, fT4, symptoms).
- **Sub-claim 2**: Hashimoto's can be "reversed" — i.e., a chronic autoimmune thyroiditis can be returned to durable, drug-free euthyroidism — and castor oil packs contribute to that.

**Pass 1 — Four lenses**

| Lens | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Safety** | Topical castor oil packs are generally low-risk (occasional contact dermatitis, oil staining of fabrics). The dominant harm is *indirect*: the framing invites viewers with established overt hypothyroidism to substitute or de-emphasize levothyroxine, the WHO Essential-Medicines-List standard of care. No absolute risk numbers, no contraindication discussion (pregnancy, menstruation, ruptured skin), and no NNT/NNH because there is no trial to derive them from. |
| **Outcomes (POEM vs DOE)** | No outcomes are quantified on-screen. The implied outcome ("reversing Hashimoto's") would be a POEM, but no patient-oriented evidence exists for castor-oil-pack-mediated remission. Even DOE-level evidence (changes in anti-TPO, TSH, fT4 attributable to castor oil packs) is absent from PubMed/Cochrane. |
| **Risk of bias** | The hook rests on personal testimonial — the lowest tier of evidence (anecdote / case report of one). The only frequently re-cited castor-oil immunology study is a 1999 double-blind trial in 36 healthy adults reporting a transient rise in T-11 (lymphocyte) values after 2-hour topical packs — healthy subjects, not Hashimoto's, no thyroid endpoints, no replication, never extended to autoimmune thyroiditis outcomes. The mechanism-to-disease leap is unwarranted. |
| **Total evidence** | The American Thyroid Association, Endocrine Society, and NICE do not list castor oil packs as an adjunct or alternative for Hashimoto's or hypothyroidism. Even functional-medicine practitioners writing in favor of the practice acknowledge in print that "there is no research which proves that castor oil packs can help to shrink thyroid nodules" (Osansky, Natural Endocrine Solutions, 2013). |

**Pass 2 — SORT grade**

- **Sub-claim 1 (castor oil packs improve Hashimoto's)**: **Grade C.** No RCTs in Hashimoto's patients; the single often-cited castor-oil immunology study (n=36 healthy adults, lymphocyte surrogate marker, no thyroid outcomes) is at most weak DOE in an unrelated population. Mechanistic / anecdotal evidence only.
- **Sub-claim 2 (Hashimoto's can be "reversed," castor oil packs contribute)**: **Grade C** for the strong form. Some adjuncts in the broader Hashimoto's nutraceutical literature (e.g., selenium) reach **Grade B** for antibody-titer reduction as a DOE alongside — not in place of — levothyroxine, but castor oil packs are not among them. Durable drug-free euthyroidism in established overt hypothyroidism is uncommon and unpredictable from any topical intervention; mainstream endocrine guidelines do not endorse "reversal" framing.

## Verdict
The Reel's entire claim — that castor oil packs help *reverse* Hashimoto's — is **Grade C**: anecdote and mechanism-only, with no Hashimoto's-specific clinical trial in the published literature, no endorsement from the ATA, Endocrine Society, or NICE, and a candid concession from sympathetic functional-medicine sources themselves that the supporting research does not exist. The pack on screen is wrapped around the abdomen (the "liver/lymph" framing), not the thyroid, which makes the leap to autoimmune thyroid reversal even more speculative. Topical castor oil itself is low-risk, so the real hazard here is the framing: viewers with overt hypothyroidism may be nudged toward replacing or downplaying levothyroxine — the only therapy with Grade A evidence for the patient-oriented outcomes that matter (symptom control, pregnancy outcomes, cardiovascular sequelae avoidance). Do not share as health guidance; if discussed at all, frame it as a personal comfort ritual, not a Hashimoto's intervention.
