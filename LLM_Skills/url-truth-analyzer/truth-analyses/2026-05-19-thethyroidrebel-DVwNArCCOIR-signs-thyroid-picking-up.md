#### Pass 1 — Four lenses

**1. Safety**
- The framing nudges patients to self-monitor for "improvement," which can encourage premature medication reduction if combined with the creator's prior content (see channel reputation note re: halved-meds testimonial).
- No on-frame disclaimer, no "talk to your doctor / repeat TSH labs" caveat visible.
- "Your thyroid is picking up" is patient-facing language for what should be confirmed by TSH/FT4 labs, not symptom self-report.

**2. Outcomes (POEM vs DOE)**
- The post promises symptom-level POEMs (energy, mood, weight, hair, etc., presumably).
- Actual recovery of thyroid function is a DOE problem (TSH, FT4, FT3, TPO antibodies). Symptom-only "recovery" without lab confirmation is unreliable — placebo, regression to the mean, and dietary co-interventions all confound symptom self-report.
- Maximum possible grade limited to **B** for any claim resting on symptom self-report alone.

**3. Risk of bias**
- No citations, no study references, no labs shown.
- Single-creator anecdotal authority; commercial coaching practice creates incentive to present optimistic recovery narratives.
- Hook-and-list reel format is designed for engagement, not precision.

**4. Total evidence**
- Hashimoto's: standard of care (ATA 2014 hypothyroidism guideline; ETA 2013) is levothyroxine titrated to TSH; "remission" is not a recognized clinical endpoint.
- Spontaneous recovery of overt hypothyroidism is rare; subclinical hypothyroidism *can* normalize without treatment in 6–62% of cases over years (depending on baseline TSH and TPO-Ab status — Diez & Iglesias, J Clin Endocrinol Metab; Huber et al.).
- AIP-diet and stress-management RCTs in Hashimoto's have shown mostly antibody/quality-of-life movement, not durable functional recovery.
