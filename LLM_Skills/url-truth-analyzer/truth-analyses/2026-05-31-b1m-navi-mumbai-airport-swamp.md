## Summary
The B1M profiles Navi Mumbai International Airport (NMIA): built on reclaimed swamp land by flattening 92 m Ulwe Hill (controlled blasting, ~62M m³ rock reused for land reclamation), diverting/widening the Ulwe River, with a Zaha Hadid lotus-inspired terminal. Phase 1 opened Dec 2025 (~20M passengers/yr), scaling to ~90M by the 2030s. Critique: opened without rail access; ~3 hr drive from some suburbs.

## Analysis

### Science: Claim Validation
Every checkable factual claim matches primary sources (The Hindu, Adani Airports, Republic World) and the publisher's own article: Dec 2025 commercial opening; 20M-passenger phase-1 capacity scaling to 90M at full build; ~1,160 hectares; Zaha Hadid lotus design; Ulwe Hill flattening and river diversion; Trans Harbour Link (Atal Setu) opened 2024. The engineering mechanism described (blasting rock to size, dumping into marsh to displace/compact soft clay, raising ground ~6 m) is standard land-reclamation practice and internally consistent. The "no rail access yet / long drive" criticism is accurate and fairly caveated as a phase-1 teething issue. Minor framing: "$2BN" aligns with the reported ₹19,650 cr phase-1 cost (~$2.3B). No misleading claims detected.
