## Analysis

### Claim Validation

**Claim 1: Forced phone lockouts (locking users out after a daily limit) reduced screen time by about 75 minutes per day.**

Status: **Partially supported** — the direction is correct, the specific number is unverified.

Multiple RCTs confirm that forced lockout interventions significantly reduce screen time. A 2025 StandUPTV full-factorial optimization trial found lockout + text + earned screen time combinations reduced recreational screen time by 125.7 minutes/day. A 2021 BMC Medicine RCT found reducing smartphone use to ≤2 hours/day improved wellbeing metrics. The 75-minute figure likely comes from a specific study cited in the podcast episode, but this exact number could not be independently verified in available literature. The magnitude is plausible given the range of effects seen across studies (60–126 min/day reductions reported).

**Claim 2: People felt "coerced and frustrated" with lockouts compared to warning messages.**

Status: **Supported** — consistent with multiple studies on autonomy and intervention design.

A 2024 University of Michigan study found that forced lockouts are "highly disruptive" — users scramble through notification screens and then lose track of time, often exceeding intended limits. The InteractOut study showed graduated friction (slowing gestures, shifting tap registration) was 16% more effective than hard lockouts and had 25.3% higher user acceptance. A 2022 nudge-based RCT (N=112) found that softer design friction (grayscale mode) reduced screen time without the negative psychological reactance that hard lockouts produce. This aligns with Self-Determination Theory: interventions that undermine autonomy (forced lockouts) tend to generate psychological reactance and lower compliance compared to those preserving user agency (nudges, warnings).
