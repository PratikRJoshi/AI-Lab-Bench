## Analysis

### Claim Validation

**Claim 1 (visible on slide 1):** "I lost 30+ lbs & 20 inches."
- **Type:** Personal anecdotal outcome (n=1 self-report).
- **Testability:** Not externally verifiable. No before/after photos, no timeline, no body-comp method (scale weight vs DEXA vs tape) shown on the visible slide.
- **Plausibility:** A 30-lb loss over many months on a sustained calorie deficit is well-documented and physiologically reasonable. 20 inches is plausible if it's a sum across multiple body sites (waist + hips + thighs + arms + chest + bust), which is the standard influencer convention for that figure. So the *number* is not implausible — but it tells us nothing about *what caused it*.
- **Verdict:** Not a falsifiable health claim; just a personal result.

**Claim 2 (teased, not visible):** "It started when I stopped THIS [pointing-finger]."
- **Type:** Causal attribution to a single habit change. The actual "THIS" is on a slide that wasn't scraped, so the specific claim cannot be evaluated.
- **General concern:** Single-cause weight-loss attributions ("stop sugar", "stop seed oils", "stop snacking", "stop rice", "stop eating after 7 pm", etc.) almost always work in practice via the same mechanism — a sustained reduction in total energy intake — rather than because the eliminated food/habit had unique metabolic effects. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses (DIETFITS, Gardner 2018; Sacks 2009 NEJM "Comparison of weight-loss diets with different compositions") consistently show macronutrient composition is a weak predictor of weight loss compared with adherence and overall calorie deficit.
- **Verdict:** Cannot evaluate without the missing slides. Generic prior: be skeptical of any "I stopped one thing and lost 30 lbs" framing — the *one thing* is rarely the mechanism; the calorie deficit is.
