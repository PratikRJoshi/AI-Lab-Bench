## Analysis

### Medical: SORT Analysis

**Distinct claim (only one in this reel):**
1. *"Cellular detoxing"* was the single intervention that lowered the creator's thyroid antibodies the most.

#### Pass 1 — Four lenses

**1. Safety lens.** No absolute risks, no number-needed-to-treat, no baseline antibody titer, no comparator, no time frame, and no specification of what "cellular detoxing" actually consists of. In this creator's broader content, "cellular detox" typically wraps several distinct interventions with different safety profiles: (a) mycotoxin binders (cholestyramine, activated charcoal, bentonite clay — can chelate thyroid hormone and other oral drugs, reducing absorption); (b) infrared sauna / forced sweating (generally low-risk but contraindicated in pregnancy at high temperatures, and in cardiac/autonomic dysfunction); (c) glutathione/NAC, sulforaphane, chlorella, zeolite (variable evidence, generally low harm at retail doses); (d) "binders + drainage" stacks marketed via affiliate links. The reel itself supplies none of this context, so a viewer cannot evaluate harm.

**2. Outcomes lens (POEM vs DOE).** "Thyroid antibodies" (anti-TPO, anti-Tg) are **disease-oriented surrogate markers (DOEs)**, not patient-oriented outcomes (POEMs). Antibody titers do not reliably track symptom burden, drug-free euthyroidism, fertility outcomes, or cardiovascular endpoints. By SORT rules, even a perfectly run RCT measuring only antibodies cannot exceed Grade B; an n=1 testimonial measuring only antibodies is far below that.

**3. Risk of bias lens.** The "evidence" presented is a single self-report from someone whose paid business sells the intervention. Study-design rank: **case report / anecdote** — the second-lowest tier above pure expert opinion. There is no control, no blinding, no pre-registration, no independent replication, and a direct financial conflict of interest. Regression-to-the-mean and natural variability in anti-TPO/Tg titers (which can fluctuate 30–50% between draws without any intervention) are unaddressed.

**4. Total evidence lens.** "Cellular detox" / "mycotoxin detox" as an intervention for autoimmune thyroiditis is **not in any major endocrine guideline** (ATA, Endocrine Society, NICE, European Thyroid Association). The interventions that *do* show modest, replicated antibody-lowering signal in Hashimoto's are: **selenium ~200 µg/day** (Grade B for the DOE of antibody reduction; Cochrane-style syntheses including PMID 39698034), **vitamin D repletion where deficient**, **gluten elimination in patients with concurrent celiac disease or NCGS**, and **anti-inflammatory diet ± curcumin** (modest signal, PMID 41329567). None of those are called "cellular detoxing." The claim that detox is *the most effective* intervention is unsupported by any randomized comparison.

#### Pass 2 — SORT grade

**Grade C** — the claim is supported only by an undisclosed-protocol n=1 testimonial from a financially conflicted source, with mechanistic hand-waving ("detox") and no RCT, systematic review, or guideline backing. Even granting the most charitable interpretation (that the underlying protocol included selenium, vitamin D, and dietary change — interventions that *do* have Grade B evidence for antibody reduction), the attribution to "cellular detoxing" specifically is not supportable; that label adds a layer of unfalsifiable narrative on top of whatever generic anti-inflammatory practices may have helped.

## Verdict
This is a one-claim Instagram reel asserting that "cellular detoxing" was the *single most effective* thing that lowered the creator's thyroid antibodies. The claim is an n=1 testimonial from someone who monetizes the exact protocol she is endorsing, the outcome is a disease-oriented surrogate marker that fluctuates on its own, the intervention itself is not defined in the reel, and no major endocrine guideline recognizes "cellular detox" / "mycotoxin detox" as a Hashimoto's intervention. SORT grade **C**. The interventions that genuinely do have replicated (Grade B, DOE) signal for lowering thyroid antibodies — selenium, vitamin D where deficient, gluten elimination in celiac/NCGS, anti-inflammatory diet — exist, but they are not what the reel is selling, and conflating them with "detox" branding does the viewer no favors. The downstream risk is the familiar one for this channel: viewers with overt hypothyroidism reading "I lowered my antibodies with detox" and inferring that they can defer or replace levothyroxine, which the ATA and Endocrine Society explicitly recommend against. Do not share.
