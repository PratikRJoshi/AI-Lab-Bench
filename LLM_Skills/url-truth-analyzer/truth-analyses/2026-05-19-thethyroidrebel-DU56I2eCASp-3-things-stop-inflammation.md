## Analysis

### Medical: SORT Analysis

**Claim 1 — "Fear of the toxins became the biggest toxin" (orthorexic clean-eating perfectionism can worsen chronic illness).**
- Outcomes lens: this is a **POEM-adjacent** psychological/QoL claim, not a surrogate-marker claim.
- Risk of bias: supported by cross-sectional and prevalence research showing orthorexia nervosa is dramatically more common in patients with chronic autoimmune/inflammatory conditions (≈75% in rheumatoid arthritis, 77% in IBD, 50% in cancer) versus ~7% in the general population, and is independently associated with health anxiety, malnutrition, and worse adherence to balanced diets. No RCTs of "letting go of purist eating" as an intervention.
- Total evidence: consistent with mainstream eating-disorder and chronic-illness psychology literature.
- **SORT Grade: B** — patient-oriented, but evidence is observational, not interventional.

**Claim 2 — "Hashimoto's is not one inflammation; it's at least four: autoimmune, metabolic, stress-driven, gut-related; single-fix protocols fail."**
- Outcomes lens: the underlying mechanistic claim (Hashimoto's involves multiple contributing pathways) is well supported — current immunology reviews describe Th17/Treg imbalance and elevated IL-17/IL-21/TNF-α/IFN-γ (autoimmune), high comorbidity with insulin resistance/metabolic syndrome (metabolic), HPA-axis/oxidative-stress contributions (stress), and altered gut microbiota plus elevated zonulin/intestinal permeability (gut). However, the specific **four-bucket taxonomy is her proprietary teaching frame, not a recognised clinical classification** (no endocrine society uses it).
- Risk of bias: the mechanistic backing is strong; the specific "you need to address all four" prescription has **no RCT** supporting the multi-track intervention as superior to standard levothyroxine + lifestyle care.
- **SORT Grade: B for the multifactorial pathogenesis. Grade C for the prescriptive "four-bucket protocol" framing.**

**Claim 3 — "A dysregulated nervous system keeps the inflammation cycle spinning no matter how perfect your diet is" → NS regulation is the "gate keeper."**
- Safety lens: framing nervous-system regulation as primary is low-risk in itself, but risks under-treatment if patients downweight evidence-based care (thyroid hormone replacement, gluten/iodine considerations, vitamin D, selenium where indicated).
- Outcomes lens: chronic psychological stress is associated with HT onset/severity in observational studies, with biologically plausible HPA-axis and oxidative-stress mechanisms. But there is **no RCT showing that nervous-system regulation interventions (vagal/somatic/breathwork) reduce TPO antibodies or improve hard thyroid outcomes** independent of pharmacotherapy.
- Risk of bias: the "gate keeper / missing piece" elevation is mechanistic extrapolation beyond the trial data.
- **SORT Grade: C** — biologically plausible, observationally supported, but no controlled patient-oriented evidence that nervous-system work is the dominant lever.

**Overall SORT Grade: B** for the broader narrative (multifactorial Hashimoto's; orthorexia harms; stress contributes); **C** for the specific "four types" and "NS is the missing piece" prescriptive framing.

## Verdict
This post is **better-than-average wellness-influencer content on Hashimoto's**: McCabe rightly pushes back on orthorexic perfectionism (supported by chronic-illness/eating-disorder literature) and rightly frames Hashimoto's as multifactorial (supported by immunology and gut-microbiome reviews). Her credentials as a registered UK nutritional therapist add some weight versus an uncredentialed influencer. Where the analysis weakens is in (a) presenting her own "4 types of inflammation" as if it were a clinical taxonomy when it is a teaching/branding device, and (b) elevating nervous-system regulation to "the missing piece" — mechanistically plausible and observationally supported, but not yet supported by RCT evidence showing it changes antibody titers or clinical thyroid outcomes independent of standard care. The post is also a lead-magnet for a paid workshop; viewers should not mistake it for guideline-based medical advice. Net SORT grade B for the broad message, C for the specific prescriptive frame.

## ELI5 — Friend to Friend
The honest parts are honest: obsessing over every label and "toxin" can stress you out enough to make a thyroid problem worse, and Hashimoto's really does have several moving parts (immune system, metabolism, stress, gut). The wobbly parts are her clean "four types" diagram and the idea that calming your nervous system is *the* fix — those are her marketing frame, not what endocrinologists say. Read it as a thoughtful coach's personal story, not as a treatment plan, and don't drop your levothyroxine or your doctor for a free workshop.
