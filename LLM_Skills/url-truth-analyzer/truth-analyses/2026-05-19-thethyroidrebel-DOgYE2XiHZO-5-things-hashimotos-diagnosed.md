## Analysis
### Medical: SORT Analysis

**Pass 1 — Four lenses**

1. **Safety**
   - The most actionable safety concern is slide 9 ("stop looking up to my GP as my saviour. They are there to prescribe meds, everything else is in my hands"). For an undiagnosed/newly-diagnosed hypothyroid patient, the GP/endocrinologist is the only person who can order TSH/FT4/FT3, prescribe and titrate levothyroxine, screen for myxedema or cardiovascular complications, and monitor in pregnancy. Reframing them as merely "med dispensers" risks under-treatment, especially in subclinical or pregnant patients where dosing matters.
   - The "reversal protocol" language is unspecified here but, taken across her catalogue, implies an end-state where exogenous thyroxine is no longer needed. Self-directed levothyroxine reduction is materially dangerous: TSH elevation in pregnancy is associated with miscarriage and impaired neurodevelopment; severe undertreatment causes myxedema coma (rare, but fatal).
   - No absolute risks, NNT, NNH, or contraindications are given for any "foundation" or "protocol" item.
   - Slide 13 (NOT doing strict elimination + 20+ supplements) is the safest claim in the deck and is actually aligned with current evidence: there is no validated benefit to extreme elimination diets or megadose stacking in Hashimoto's, and harm (orthorexia, deficiencies, hepatotoxicity from supplements) is documented.

2. **Outcomes (POEM vs DOE)**
   - The implicit headline outcome is "reverse hypothyroidism and its symptoms." This is a POEM-class claim (symptom resolution, drug discontinuation), and the bar for it is high.
   - The cited *mechanism* claims are DOE/surrogate at best: reducing TPO antibodies (DOE), lowering inflammation (mechanistic), "putting out the fire" (metaphor).
   - No specific outcome data — no remission rates, no antibody trajectories, no levothyroxine-discontinuation rates, no quality-of-life scores — are presented to support the implied POEM.
   - The "foundation" items (sleep, circadian, blood sugar, nervous-system regulation) do have POEM evidence for *general* fatigue, mood, and metabolic health, but no RCT links them causally to Hashimoto's reversal or to discontinuation of thyroid replacement.

3. **Risk of bias**
   - Zero citations on the slides. Single source = the creator, who is selling a paid course funneled directly from the final slide.
   - Strong commercial conflict of interest: the post's terminal CTA (£33 with promo code) is the actual purpose; the educational content is the on-ramp.
   - Selection/confirmation bias in framing: "see Hashimoto's as a GOOD THING because we can reverse it" presupposes the very claim under test.
   - Anti-physician framing (slide 9) is a recognized red flag in alt-health marketing — it preemptively immunizes the audience against contrary clinical advice.

4. **Total evidence**
   - **Full thyroid panel including FT3 + TPO/TgAb (slide 2)**: Reasonable and aligned with ATA/AACE practice for new presentations or suspected autoimmunity (though NHS standard is TSH first, reflex FT4, antibodies if indicated). Defensible.
   - **Hashimoto's is "reversible" (slide 8 and the whole protocol premise)**: Not supported by current evidence. Selenium meta-analyses show modest TPO-antibody reductions (a DOE) without consistent improvement in thyroid function or symptoms; gluten-free trials in non-celiac Hashimoto's show small antibody effects but no clinical/POEM benefit; lifestyle interventions improve general well-being but no RCT or systematic review demonstrates durable remission off levothyroxine. NICE NG145 and ATA guidelines describe Hashimoto's hypothyroidism as chronic.
   - **Foundations: circadian, sleep, blood sugar, nervous-system support (slide 10)**: Individually evidence-based for fatigue and metabolic health; aligned with adjunctive care. The specific causal claim that fixing these *reverses* Hashimoto's is unsupported.
   - **Mindset / health-anxiety work (slide 12)**: CBT/ACT for health anxiety has POEM support (reduced distress, improved function); positioning it as part of "healing" the autoimmune disease itself is mechanistically overclaimed.
   - **"NOT strict elimination diets and 20+ supplements" (slide 13)**: Correct and the strongest slide in the deck — consistent with EBM, which finds no benefit from megadose supplement stacks in autoimmune thyroiditis and documents harm.
   - **Anti-GP framing (slide 9)**: Contradicts every major guideline; standard of care for hypothyroidism is GP/endocrinology-led levothyroxine titration with periodic monitoring.

**Pass 2 — Grade**

**Grade C — overall.** The aggregate claim ("you can reverse Hashimoto's through my protocol") rests on mechanistic reasoning, expert (self-) opinion, and DOE-level evidence at best; no RCTs or systematic reviews support remission/discontinuation as a reproducible outcome of any nutrition/lifestyle protocol. Two component sub-claims earn higher local grades — the "don't megadose" warning (slide 13, B: aligned with consensus and a plausibility-of-harm argument) and "get a full thyroid panel with antibodies" (slide 2, B: defensible per ATA/AACE) — but the headline reversal claim and the anti-GP framing pull the post's overall grade to C.

## Verdict
Treat this carousel as marketing for a £33 live training, not as clinical guidance. Two slides are reasonable on their own — get a full thyroid panel including antibodies, and don't throw yourself at extreme elimination diets and a 20-supplement stack — but the headline narrative that Hashimoto's is a reversible condition you can resolve through her "root-cause protocol" is not supported by current evidence (Grade C). The slide that most concerns me is the anti-GP framing: for hypothyroidism, your GP isn't an optional accessory to medication, they are the person ensuring you don't get under- or over-treated, especially around pregnancy and cardiovascular risk. Lifestyle, sleep, blood sugar regulation, and stress work are all genuinely useful adjuncts — just not as substitutes for guideline-directed care, and not as proof that an autoimmune disease has been "cured."
