## Analysis

### Science: Claim Validation
Disorganized attachment is characterized in the literature by a simultaneous wanting and fearing of closeness — an approach-avoidance conflict often linked to frightening/inconsistent early caregiving and trauma [1]. The reel's description ("want closeness then pull away when it gets vulnerable") matches this well. The central prescription — consistent, predictable, safe presence as the agent of change — aligns with co-regulation evidence: a trusted partner's steady presence measurably buffers the threat response, and the effect is strongest in higher-quality, secure-feeling bonds [2]. The "pattern over time, not the perfect moment" framing matches findings that attachment change is gradual and repetition-driven [3]. Importantly, the reel explicitly states the disorganized partner still owns their healing work, avoiding the harmful trope that one partner can single-handedly "fix" the other. Caveat: disorganized attachment is frequently trauma-linked, and the reel doesn't mention professional support, which is often warranted.

## ELI5 — Friend to Friend
If your partner both craves closeness and bolts when it gets real, that's the "disorganized" pattern, usually rooted in a scary or unpredictable past. The thing that actually helps is being boringly consistent and safe over and over — not saying one perfect thing. This reel gets it right, and it's honest that they have to do their own work too. If trauma's involved, nudge them toward a therapist; love alone isn't a treatment plan.
