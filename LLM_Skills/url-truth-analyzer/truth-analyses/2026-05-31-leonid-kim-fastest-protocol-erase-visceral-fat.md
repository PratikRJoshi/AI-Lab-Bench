## Analysis

### Medical: SORT Analysis
The framing that calorie quality (not just quantity) affects *visceral* fat is well-supported and the headline example is real: the **DIRECT-PLUS** trial showed a green/polyphenol-rich Mediterranean diet (walnuts + green tea + Mankai duckweed) produced roughly double the MRI-measured visceral/intrahepatic fat loss vs a standard Mediterranean diet at matched calories (C2). The mechanistic claims are individually evidence-backed: omega-3s/polyphenols lower adipose inflammation; sulforaphane activates NRF2 and suppresses hepatic gluconeogenesis (the Axelsson 2017 sulforaphane/fasting-glucose work); EVOO polyphenols engage AMPK/SIRT1. The olive-oil-vs-soybean-oil "80% more fat loss" (C4) is a single small RCT — directionally consistent but should not be over-read. The exercise section repeats the well-supported finding (vigorous aerobic/HIIT best for visceral fat; dose-dependent; scale can stay flat). Appropriately hedged; encourages gradual intensity ramp and MD clearance for cardiac patients.
