### Medical: SORT Analysis

**Grade: C — no specific claims extractable from this post.**

Per the project's hard rule for empty/degenerate transcripts paired with hook-only thumbnails: rather than infer a list of "perimenopause-thyroid signs" the creator *might* have spoken and grade those, this post is flagged as un-gradable. What can be said about the underlying topic:

- **Topic is clinically real.** Thyroid dysfunction (subclinical hypothyroidism in particular) becomes more prevalent in women across the 40–55 perimenopausal window, and its symptoms — fatigue, sleep disruption, mood changes, menstrual irregularity, hair shedding, thermoregulatory issues — overlap heavily with vasomotor and other perimenopausal symptoms. This overlap is well documented (EMAS position statement, 2024; Uygur et al. 2018; Pearce 2007).
- **Pass 1 — four lenses on the *topic* (not on this specific post's unspoken claims):**
  - *Safety:* The differential matters because untreated overt hypothyroidism in mid-life carries real cardiovascular and quality-of-life risk; conversely, attributing genuine perimenopause symptoms to "low thyroid" can drive unnecessary supplementation or T3/desiccated-thyroid use in euthyroid women, which has its own harms.
  - *Outcomes:* TSH-screening of mid-life women with non-specific symptoms is a Grade B clinical practice (POEM for cases where overt disease is found; mostly DOE — normalizing labs — in subclinical disease without symptom benefit).
  - *Risk of bias:* Wellness-influencer "sneaky signs" lists tend to be high-sensitivity / low-specificity (almost any tired perimenopausal woman will match ≥3 signs), pushing viewers toward self-diagnosis.
  - *Total evidence:* EMAS (2024) and ATA guidance recommend TSH measurement when symptoms are ambiguous in mid-life women; they do *not* endorse a fixed "checklist of signs" approach, and they caution against over-treatment of mild subclinical hypothyroidism.
- **Pass 2 — Grade: C for this post.** No extractable claims; topic legitimacy ≠ post legitimacy.
