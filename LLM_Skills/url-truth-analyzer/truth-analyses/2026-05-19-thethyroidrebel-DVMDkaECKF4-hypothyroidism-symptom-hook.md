## Analysis
### Medical: SORT Analysis

**Extractable claim (single, hook-level)**: "Recognising ≥3 'of these' symptoms means it is not *just* hypothyroidism."

The implied symptom list is not visible on the supplied cover frame (it would appear in subsequent video frames the analysis pipeline did not capture as a still). Therefore no specific symptom-to-mechanism claims can be evaluated. What *can* be evaluated is the framing premise.

**Pass 1 — Four lenses (on the framing premise)**

- **Safety**: Low direct harm from a symptom-recognition hook itself. Indirect harm risk: viewers convinced they have an undertreated "something more than hypothyroidism" may pursue self-funded private testing/supplements, delay or distrust GP-prescribed levothyroxine, or self-medicate with desiccated thyroid / T3. No safety data given in-post; no NNT/NNH discussion (none expected in a hook).
- **Outcomes**: No outcomes claimed. Pure recognition prompt. POEM/DOE distinction does not apply yet.
- **Risk of bias**: Personal-brand creator with a paid coaching offer; the hook is calibrated to drive engagement and eventual conversion to her services. Selection bias in which symptoms will be listed is essentially guaranteed (broad, high-prevalence symptoms maximise self-identification).
- **Total evidence**: The premise "if you have hypothyroid symptoms it's probably more than 'just' hypothyroidism" is partially defensible because ~90% of primary hypothyroidism in iodine-sufficient countries *is* autoimmune (Hashimoto's), and a meaningful minority of treated patients (10–15%) report persistent symptoms on adequate levothyroxine (Wiersinga 2014; Hennessey 2018). However, mainstream endocrinology already recognises both facts — antibody testing is routine, and persistent-symptom management is in the ATA 2014 guideline. The "your doctor is missing this" implication is therefore overstated against current standard care.

**Pass 2 — Grade**: **C** — no specific clinical claim is testable from the supplied frame; the framing premise rests on expert/marketing opinion plus selectively presented epidemiology, not on patient-oriented RCT evidence.

## Verdict
The cover-frame hook by itself does not contain a falsifiable medical claim, so a strict SORT grade lands at **C**. The implicit premise — that having "≥3 symptoms" means hypothyroidism alone doesn't explain your picture — is partially true (most primary hypothyroidism is autoimmune Hashimoto's, and 10–15% of treated patients have residual symptoms), but it is packaged as a discovery the creator is uniquely positioned to explain, which is not accurate: routine NHS / endocrinology care already covers antibody testing and residual-symptom workup. Without the in-video symptom list there is no further-specific claim to verify; users should treat this as engagement bait into a paid nutrition-coaching funnel, not as a clinical screening tool.
