## Analysis

### EBM SORT Analysis

**Pass 1: Four Analytical Lenses**

**Safety Lens:**
- **No absolute values reported**: "amazing result" and "didn't reduce sugar spike by that much" are relative statements without baseline glucose levels, peak values, or time-to-peak
- **No context on individual variability**: CGM responses vary significantly between individuals based on insulin sensitivity, metabolic health, meal timing, prior activity
- **Missing meal composition details**: How much paneer bhurji? Total calories? Carbohydrate content? Cooking method (oil quantity)?
- **No mention of limitations**: Single-subject experiment (n=1), no control for confounders (time of day, prior meals, physical activity), no statistical analysis

**Outcomes Lens:**
- **Glucose spike is a DOE**: Post-prandial glucose is a surrogate marker, not a patient-oriented outcome. Lower glucose excursions correlate with but don't guarantee improved long-term health (HbA1c, cardiovascular risk, diabetes prevention)
- **Short-term measurement**: CGM shows 2-3 hour response, not sustained effects or metabolic adaptation
- **No clinical endpoint**: Video shows graphs but doesn't discuss symptoms, satiety, weight, or health status

**Risk of Bias Lens:**
- **Single-subject case report**: N=1 observational study with no control group
- **No blinding**: Subject knows what they're eating and likely has expectations about outcomes
- **Cherry-picking risk**: "Let's hope for a few more good results" suggests multiple experiments — only successes may be shared
- **Lack of standardization**: No mention of meal timing, portion sizes, or CGM calibration
- **Influencer context**: Instagram health content creator — engagement incentives may favor dramatic or positive results

**Total Evidence Lens:**
- **Protein/fat blunting carb response**: WELL-ESTABLISHED. Adding protein and fat to carbohydrate-rich meals slows gastric emptying and reduces glucose spike. Meta-analyses confirm this effect
- **Paneer (cottage cheese) specifically**: HIGH PROTEIN (~18-20g per 100g), HIGH FAT (~20-25g per 100g), NEGLIGIBLE CARBS — ideal macronutrient profile for blunting glucose
- **Dal and sabzi comparison**: Dal has protein but also significant carbs (20-25g per cup cooked). Sabzi varies widely but typically lower protein. Paneer's higher fat content likely explains superior glucose-blunting effect
- **Mechanism**: Fat slows gastric emptying, protein stimulates incretin hormones (GLP-1), both reduce postprandial glycemia. This is textbook physiology, not novel

**Pass 2: SORT Grade Assignment**

**Grade: C (Consensus, disease-oriented evidence only)**

**Justification:**
1. **N=1 case report**: Single individual's CGM trace is anecdotal evidence, not generalizable research
2. **DOE only**: Glucose spike is a surrogate marker. True patient outcomes (HbA1c reduction, diabetes prevention, cardiovascular risk) are not measured
3. **Well-established mechanism**: The underlying principle (protein/fat blunt carb response) is Grade A evidence from RCTs, but this specific experiment adds no new evidence — it demonstrates an expected physiological response
4. **No peer review or replication**: Instagram video of personal experiment, not published study
5. **Missing methodological rigor**: No control for confounders, no statistical analysis, no quantitative comparison (peak glucose values, AUC)

**Why not Grade B:**
- No patient-oriented outcomes
- Not a clinical trial or systematic observation — just personal CGM documentation
- No attempt at controlling variables or replication

**Why not Grade A:**
- Single anecdotal observation, not RCT or meta-analysis
- Surrogate endpoint only

**Context: What the underlying evidence says (Grade A):**
- Multiple RCTs and meta-analyses confirm that co-ingesting protein/fat with carbohydrates reduces post-prandial glucose excursions by 20-40%
- This effect is dose-dependent and reproducible across populations
- Long-term health benefits of this strategy for metabolic syndrome and T2DM prevention are supported but less robust (Grade B)

## Verdict

Rohan Sehgal's paneer bhurji experiment is **scientifically expected but anecdotally presented**. The observation that adding high-protein, high-fat paneer to a meal reduces glucose spike compared to dal (moderate protein, moderate carbs) or sabzi (lower protein) aligns perfectly with established nutritional physiology. The mechanism — fat slowing gastric emptying, protein stimulating insulin and incretins — is supported by Grade A evidence from RCTs and meta-analyses.

However, this specific video earns Grade C because: (1) it's a single-subject observation (n=1), (2) glucose spike is a surrogate endpoint not a health outcome, (3) no methodological rigor (no controls, no quantification, potential cherry-picking), and (4) presented as entertainment/engagement content rather than systematic research.

**Practical takeaway:** The advice is sound — adding paneer (or other high-protein, high-fat foods like eggs, full-fat yogurt, nuts) to carb-heavy meals will reduce glucose spikes for most people. This strategy is particularly relevant for individuals with insulin resistance, prediabetes, or T2DM. But viewers should understand this is demonstrating known physiology, not discovering new science. For personalized guidance, use your own CGM data or consult a dietitian rather than relying on influencer experiments.

**Missing for actionability:** Portion sizes, total meal macros, comparison metrics (peak glucose, area under curve), individual variability discussion, long-term sustainability vs short-term glucose effect.
