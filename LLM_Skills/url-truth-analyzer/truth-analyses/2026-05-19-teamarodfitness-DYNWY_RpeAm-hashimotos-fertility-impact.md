## Analysis

### SORT Analysis

**Pass 1 — Four lenses**

- **Safety**: The post implicitly discourages "conventional treatment" for Hashimoto's. This is the single biggest harm vector: untreated overt hypothyroidism in pregnancy is itself associated with miscarriage, preterm birth, and adverse neurodevelopment. Levothyroxine for overt hypothyroidism is one of the highest-evidence interventions in obstetrics. No absolute risks are reported (no "X% of TPO-Ab+ women miscarry vs Y% of antibody-negative women"); the claim is delivered as personal certainty. Side effects, contraindications, and the possibility that her three losses were due to other (very common) causes — chromosomal aneuploidy, anatomic factors, age, antiphospholipid syndrome, etc. — are not mentioned. Recurrent pregnancy loss workup is a defined clinical pathway (ASRM guideline), and Hashimoto's is one of *many* items on it, not the foregone conclusion presented here.

- **Outcomes (POEM vs DOE)**: The hooks are POEMs (live birth, miscarriage, fertility) — patient-relevant. But the *causal* attribution of those POEMs to Hashimoto's, and the implicit POEM promised by "the right strategies and protocols," are *not* backed by patient-oriented trial evidence. The TABLET RCT (the largest and best trial in this exact population — euthyroid TPO-Ab+ women with prior miscarriage or infertility) found *no* live-birth benefit from levothyroxine vs placebo (37.4% vs 37.9%, RR 0.97, p=0.74). If even thyroid hormone replacement does not move the live-birth needle in this group, the bar for any non-pharmacologic "protocol" claiming fertility restoration is correspondingly high — and that bar has not been met.

- **Risk of bias**: This is a single n=1 anecdote from someone who sells the recommended alternative. There is no comparator, no blinding, no pre-registration, severe selection bias (the creator's own retold story), and a very large undisclosed financial conflict of interest. On the bias hierarchy this sits below "case series" and at "anecdote with COI."

- **Total evidence**: The association between TPO antibodies and miscarriage *is* real and supported by meta-analysis (Thangaratinam 2011 BMJ, ~2× pooled OR), so the post is not pulled from thin air. But the inference chain — *TPO-Ab+ raises miscarriage risk on average → therefore Hashimoto's caused my three losses → therefore conventional treatment is useless → therefore my protocol works* — collapses at every step after the first. The "no conventional treatment" claim is flatly inconsistent with the body of literature on levothyroxine for Hashimoto's-induced hypothyroidism (the standard, effective replacement therapy) and with selenium / NSAID immunomodulation literature where it's been studied.

**Pass 2 — Grade**

**Grade C.** The therapeutic claim ("you can get better with the right strategies and protocols") rests on personal anecdote, expert (self-)opinion, and mechanistic hand-waving. The strongest patient-oriented trial in the closest matched population (TABLET) is *negative*, which means the strongest pharmacologic intervention for the precise problem doesn't change live births — and there is no comparable RCT for any "functional nutrition protocol" claiming fertility restoration in Hashimoto's. The descriptive claim that TPO-Ab is associated with miscarriage is supported (would be Grade B in isolation), but the post's actionable takeaway is not.

**Specific factual ratings**

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Hashimoto's is progressive" | Partially supported. Subclinical → overt hypothyroidism conversion runs ~2–5%/yr, higher with TPO-Ab+. With levothyroxine, life expectancy is normal. "Until it consumes you" is hyperbole. |
| "Hashimoto's robbed me of three babies" | Plausible association, not established causation. TPO-Ab+ ~2× miscarriage risk on average; individual attribution requires excluding the many other RPL causes. |
| "Robs you of your fertility all together" | Overstated. Most women with Hashimoto's, especially when euthyroid on replacement, conceive and carry to term. |
| "There is no conventional treatment for Hashimoto's" | **False as stated.** Levothyroxine replacement for the resulting hypothyroidism is the standard, well-evidenced treatment. There is no curative immunomodulator for the autoimmune process itself, which is a different (narrower) statement. |
| "You can get better with the right strategies and protocols" | Unsupported for fertility outcomes. Small AIP/gut-protocol pilot studies show some quality-of-life improvement; no RCT shows live-birth benefit. |

## Verdict
The grief is real and the underlying biology is partially real (TPO antibodies are associated with higher miscarriage risk). Everything beyond that — the categorical attribution of three specific losses, the "no conventional treatment" line, and the implication that her paid protocol fixes fertility — is either factually wrong or unsupported by the trials that have actually asked the question. The single best RCT in this exact population (TABLET) shows that even levothyroxine doesn't move live births; no functional-nutrition protocol has produced equal or better evidence. The post's main practical effect is to push grieving women away from standard endocrine care and toward a sales funnel run by a non-clinician with an explicit financial interest. **Grade C, share-no.**

## ELI5 — Friend to Friend
She's not lying that thyroid antibodies can raise miscarriage risk a bit — that part's real. But "Hashimoto's caused all three losses and the only way out is my program" is a leap. The biggest, best study ever done on this exact group (women with thyroid antibodies and miscarriages) tested the obvious thyroid drug and found it didn't help live births. So if even the real medicine doesn't fix it, a coach selling protocols definitely hasn't proven hers does. If you or someone you love is going through pregnancy losses, see a maternal-fetal medicine doc / RPL clinic, not Instagram.
