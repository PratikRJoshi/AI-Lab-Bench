## Analysis

### Science: Claim Validation
The claim is largely normative (advice), but its empirical backbone is solid. Gottman's longitudinal research identifies criticism and contempt among the strongest behavioral predictors of relationship dissolution [1], and EFT explicitly frames blame/criticism as part of the negative cycle that maintains distress, not resolves it [2]. The "plant" metaphor maps onto the well-supported idea that felt safety, not pressure, enables relational change. This is an accurate, harmless reframing; the only caveat is that an aphorism necessarily oversimplifies (there's a difference between toxic shaming and honest accountability, which the post doesn't unpack).

## Verdict
There is only one claim (C1), and it is well supported: decades of observational and treatment research show that criticism/contempt corrode relationships while safety enables change [1][2]. The weakness is the format — an aphorism can't distinguish destructive shaming from constructive accountability, so a reader could over-apply it to avoid all hard feedback. Recommend "Yes" to share: it's accurate, harmless, and directionally helpful. Both citations are title-match verified rather than directly fetched.

## ELI5 — Friend to Friend
Nagging or shaming your partner into changing basically never works — it just makes them defensive, and research backs that up. Safety and warmth are what actually let people grow. This post is a nice, true reminder. Next time you're frustrated, try naming what you need instead of attacking who they are.
