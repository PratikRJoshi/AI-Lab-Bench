## Analysis

### SORT Analysis

**Primary Claim: "Calories in calories out was never designed for a Hashimoto's body"**

This is the same claim analyzed in the DXpG1ljm6dk carousel but presented with more context here. The expanded framing adds:

1. "Your metabolism isn't just 'slow'" — This is partially true. Hashimoto's causes genuine hypothyroidism, which reduces BMR. However, once treated with levothyroxine to achieve normal TSH/FT4, metabolic rate normalizes in most patients.

2. "You're not eating too much or moving too little" — Unfalsifiable claim. Self-reported dietary intake is notoriously inaccurate (studies show 30-50% underreporting in overweight populations).

3. "The problem isn't your discipline" — Emotionally validating but scientifically misleading. If someone is in true caloric deficit and not losing weight, the differential includes: water retention, medication effects, measurement error, or metabolic adaptation — not "CICO doesn't apply."

#### Evidence Assessment
- **For the claim**: Untreated or undertreated hypothyroidism does reduce BMR by ~100-300 kcal/day. This is real and makes weight loss harder, but doesn't suspend thermodynamics.
- **Against the claim**: Multiple studies (Jonklaas 2014, Samuels 2018) show that adequately treated Hashimoto's patients have normal energy expenditure. Weight-loss interventions work normally once thyroid replacement is optimized.
- **Missing context**: The creator never mentions medication optimization as the primary intervention. This omission steers viewers toward her alternative protocols rather than standard-of-care thyroid replacement therapy.

### SORT Grade: C
Expert opinion contradicting established physiology. No evidence that energy balance is suspended in any human population including Hashimoto's patients.
