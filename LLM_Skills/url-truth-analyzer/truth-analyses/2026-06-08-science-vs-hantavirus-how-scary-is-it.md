## Analysis

### Science / EBM
The historical and transmission facts are correct. Before 1996, hantavirus transmission was considered strictly rodent-to-human (dead-end human infections); the 1996 El Bolsón, Argentina outbreak provided the first molecular/epidemiologic evidence of person-to-person spread, and **Andes virus (ANDV) is the only hantavirus known to transmit person-to-person** [2][3]. WHO and CDC confirm this transmission is limited and tied to close/prolonged contact (households, intimate partners, unprotected caregiving), most likely in the early symptomatic phase — not efficient community spread [1][2]. Documented clusters have generally been small (the cited Chubut 2018 superspreading event reached 34 confirmed cases) [3], consistent with "a couple dozen." The "~2% seroprevalence / 1 in 50" figure aligns with the existence of asymptomatic and undiagnosed infections (seroprevalence is an active research area); treat the precise 2% as the show's episode figure rather than a single canonical statistic, but the qualitative claim (many infections go unnoticed) is sound. The COVID contrast — a genuinely *new* virus then vs. a 3-decade-studied one now — is fair.
