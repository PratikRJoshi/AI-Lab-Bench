## Analysis

### Science / EBM
All three reasons and both reassurances are well-supported:
- **Strength/performance**: creatine increases muscle phosphocreatine, improving short, high-intensity output — the best-established ergogenic benefit [1][2]. ✔
- **Muscle growth (indirect)**: she correctly frames it as "not magic" — better training capacity and recovery → stronger growth stimulus, which is the accurate mechanism (vs. implying creatine builds muscle directly) [1][2]. ✔
- **Brain/cognition/mood**: ISSN documents raised brain creatine (~5–15%), reduced mental fatigue, blunted cognitive decline under sleep deprivation; emerging aging/mood research, with possible female-specific benefit [1][2][3]. ✔ ("emerging research" is appropriately hedged.)
- **Not a steroid**: correct — creatine is an amino-acid-derived compound, not an anabolic steroid [2]. ✔
- **Safe 3–5 g/day in healthy individuals**: matches ISSN safety consensus [1][2]. ✔

Scientifically clean and appropriately caveated. The only non-neutral element is the brand promotion.
