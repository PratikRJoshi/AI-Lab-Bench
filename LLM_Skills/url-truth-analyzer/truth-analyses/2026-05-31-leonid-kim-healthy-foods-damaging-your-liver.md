## Analysis

### Medical: SORT Analysis
The central mechanism — excess fructose is preferentially metabolized by the liver into fat (de novo lipogenesis), and liquid/rapid fructose overwhelms the small intestine's clearance capacity so it "spills over" to the liver — reflects current metabolic-hepatology research (the intestinal-fructose-clearance/"spillover" model and DNL are well-established). Specific supports cited (a ~7-day high-fructose AJCN study raising liver fat; ~9-day fructose-restriction lowering liver fat) are consistent with the published fructose-restriction literature (e.g., Schwarz/Lustig pediatric work). The added-sugar figures and AHA daily limits (36 g men / 25 g women) are accurate. The leptin point (fructose doesn't trigger satiety like glucose) is supported. The practical framing — the problem is processing/concentration (removing fiber, adding sugar) more than any single "bad food" — is accurate and non-alarmist (he repeatedly says whole fruit is fine). NAFLD reversibility with dietary change is well-supported.
