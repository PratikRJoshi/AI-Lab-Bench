## Analysis

### SORT Analysis

**Claim**: If you've been gluten-free for 2 years and still have daily bloating, gluten was never the cause.

#### Safety Lens
- The claim implicitly discourages gluten-free diets for people with persistent bloating. For patients with confirmed celiac disease, this could be harmful if misinterpreted as permission to reintroduce gluten.
- No mention of celiac disease screening (tTG-IgA), which should be ruled out before concluding gluten isn't the issue.
- No discussion of incomplete gluten avoidance (cross-contamination is common and can sustain symptoms).

#### Outcomes Lens
- The claim uses a patient-oriented outcome (bloating/symptom persistence) but no evidence is cited.
- The logic is: persistent symptom despite intervention → intervention targeted wrong cause. This is valid reasoning in principle but ignores: incomplete adherence, concurrent conditions, or partial response.

#### Risk of Bias Lens
- **Study design**: None cited. This is purely anecdotal/clinical opinion.
- **Conflict of interest**: The creator sells coaching programs that offer an alternative approach to Hashimoto's management. The narrative "standard approaches failed you, I have the answer" is a common marketing framework.
- **Cherry-picking**: Selects only the scenario where gluten-free didn't work, ignoring the substantial evidence that gluten-free diets do help many Hashimoto's patients (particularly those with concurrent celiac, which affects ~5% of Hashimoto's patients vs 1% general population).

#### Total Evidence Lens
- Non-celiac gluten sensitivity (NCGS) is a real but poorly understood condition. A 2019 systematic review (Roszkowska et al., Nutrients) found that ~6% of the general population reports NCGS symptoms.
- Bloating has many causes: SIBO, functional dyspepsia, gastroparesis, bile acid malabsorption, carbohydrate malabsorption (FODMAPs), ovarian pathology, etc.
- The claim that "it was never the gluten" is an unfalsifiable absolute. For some patients it IS the gluten; for others it isn't. The blanket statement is misleading.
- Evidence shows Hashimoto's patients have higher rates of celiac (~4-5x general population) — so gluten IS more likely to be relevant in this specific population, not less.

### SORT Grade: C
Expert opinion / clinical anecdote with no cited evidence. The claim makes a sweeping generalization that contradicts the known elevated celiac prevalence in Hashimoto's populations.

## Verdict
The message contains a grain of truth — bloating has many causes beyond gluten, and some people adopt gluten-free diets unnecessarily. However, stating "it was never the gluten" as a blanket claim to Hashimoto's patients is irresponsible given the 4-5x elevated celiac prevalence in this population. The post functions primarily as a marketing hook (your current approach failed → I have the real answer) rather than evidence-based education.

## ELI5 — Friend to Friend
It's complicated. She's right that bloating has tons of causes and going gluten-free doesn't magically fix everything. But telling Hashimoto's patients specifically that "it was never the gluten" is sketchy — those patients are actually MORE likely to have real gluten problems than the average person. This is basically an ad for her coaching: "everything else failed you, come to me." Take the "explore other causes" part, ignore the absolute "never the gluten" claim.
