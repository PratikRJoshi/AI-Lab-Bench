## Analysis

### Science: Claim Validation
This is one of the more empirically testable posts, and it holds up well. Attachment research supports a "prototype" model: early patterns are influential but modifiable — Fraley's (2002) meta-analysis found only moderate stability (r≈.39) from infancy to adulthood [1], and Waters et al. (2000) found ~28% of people changed attachment classification over 20 years [2]. A large naturalistic study (n>4,000) found ~25% of major life events produce enduring shifts, often toward greater security [3]. The "earned secure" literature documents people who develop security in adulthood despite insecure childhoods via corrective relationships and therapy [1][2]. The reframing from fixed identity ("an anxious person") to learned, changeable pattern is both accurate and clinically protective against fatalism. Minor caveat: change is typically gradual and partial, not a switch you flip — the short post understandably omits that nuance.

## ELI5 — Friend to Friend
Good news the science actually backs: "I'm just an anxious/avoidant person" isn't a permanent label — it's a habit you picked up to feel safe, and habits can change. Studies tracking people for decades show a real chunk of us shift toward secure over time, especially in good relationships or therapy. So don't write yourself off. Pick one small secure behavior to practice repeatedly — repetition is how it rewires.
