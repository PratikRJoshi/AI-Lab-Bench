## Summary
Three no-electricity ways to stay cool: (1) **Vetiver/khus ("vada") roots** in your water pot — natural "infused water," claimed to also help headaches, acne, dandruff, and to cool the air in coolers/curtains; (2) **Mehendi (henna)** on the hands to cool the body and de-stress via its fragrance; (3) **Chhaas (buttermilk)** 2–3×/day — has protein, calcium, B12, acts as prebiotic/probiotic/postbiotic, and with salt/cumin/asafoetida restores electrolytes lost to heat and aids digestion.

## Analysis

### Science: Claim Validation
**Chaas/buttermilk (C3) is the strongest claim.** Per USDA-based data, one cup of cultured buttermilk supplies ~8 g protein, ~22% DV calcium, ~22% DV vitamin B12, riboflavin, and live cultures (probiotics); it's ~90% water with potassium/sodium electrolytes, making it a legitimately good rehydration/digestion drink in heat [1][3]. Calling it "prebiotic, probiotic, AND postbiotic" is loose marketing language — it's primarily a probiotic fermented dairy — but the nutrition/hydration substance is correct.

**Vetiver/khus (C1)** is a centuries-old Indian summer coolant (khus sharbat, cooler pads, window mats). Its cooling is real but **physical/evaporative and sensory** (fragrant evaporative cooling of air; pleasant aroma), not a demonstrated drop in core body temperature from drinking khus water [4]. The bolt-on claims that it treats headaches, acne, and dandruff are traditional/anecdotal with no strong clinical trials cited.

**Mehendi/henna (C2)** "cools the body" is a folk belief; any effect is local/perceptual (and the de-stress claim is about ritual and fragrance). No solid clinical evidence that henna lowers body temperature.

### Net
One evidence-backed item (buttermilk) bundled with two pleasant, low-risk traditional remedies whose "cooling" is sensory/evaporative rather than physiologically proven.

## Verdict
Strongest: **C3** — buttermilk is a genuinely good, electrolyte- and protein-rich summer drink (USDA-based) [1][3]. Weakest: **C2** — henna "cooling the body" is folk belief with no clinical support. The khus claims (C1) describe a real traditional coolant but overstate ingestible/medicinal benefits. None of it is dangerous, so it's fine to try; just treat khus/mehendi as pleasant tradition, not medicine. Buttermilk citations verified directly; khus/henna rest on traditional (non-clinical) sources, which is why those grades are low.

## ELI5 — Friend to Friend
Of the three: chaas (buttermilk) is the real winner — it's basically a natural electrolyte drink with protein and gut-friendly bacteria, great in a heatwave. Khus water and mehendi feel cooling and smell lovely, but that's mostly your senses, not your core temperature dropping — nice tradition, not medicine. Drink the chaas; enjoy the khus and henna for vibes.
