## Summary

This video explains how gut microbes influence brain function via the microbiome-gut-brain axis. Key claims: the enteric nervous system (ENS, "second brain") comprises 500 million neurons and communicates with the brain via the vagus nerve; the microbiome can influence brain function by producing neurotransmitters, affecting the vagus nerve, and modulating the immune system; stress alters gut microbiome composition (shown in mice); microbiome changes are bidirectional (gut affects brain, brain affects gut); germ-free mice studies show abnormal stress responses that can be corrected by microbiome transplant; and early-life microbiome is shaped by birth mode (vaginal vs. C-section), breastfeeding, and antibiotics.
