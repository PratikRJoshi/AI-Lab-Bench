## Analysis

### SORT Analysis (EBM)

**Overall grade: A** for the central educational claims; the program-promotion paragraph is opinion, not evidence-graded.

#### Pass 1 — Four lenses

**Safety.** No drug, dosage, or procedural recommendation is made. The lifestyle interventions named (nutrition, strength training, sleep, stress management) are low-risk and have a favorable safety profile. The post explicitly disavows "crash diets or starvation," which is appropriate — energy-restriction extremes can worsen menstrual dysfunction and disordered eating in PMOS/PCOS patients. No safety harms identified.

**Outcomes.** Claims mix patient-oriented (POEM) and disease-oriented (DOE) endpoints. Improvement in **insulin sensitivity** is a DOE on its own, but in PMOS/PCOS that DOE is anchored to multiple POEMs in the literature: restoration of ovulation, menstrual regularity, fertility (Lim 2019 Cochrane), reduced progression to type 2 diabetes (Moran 2010), and improvements in androgenic skin/hair symptoms. The post frames the goal as "not temporary weight loss" but sustainable habit change — also a POEM-aligned framing.

**Risks of bias.** Two structural biases to flag:
1. *Commercial conflict.* Slides 11 and 13 promote NewME, the creator's paid program. This is disclosed implicitly (the handle is `@dr.pals_newme`) but a viewer should weight the "this is how you treat it" recommendation against that fact.
2. *Specialty mismatch.* Dr. Pal is a gastroenterologist, not an endocrinologist or reproductive medicine specialist. The PMOS rename is real and his framing is accurate, but the deepest expert authority on PMOS lies elsewhere.

No cherry-picked single-study citation is shown. No misleading statistics or relative-risk inflation. The post does not cite the 2026 Lancet consensus paper directly, which would have strengthened it.

**Total evidence.** The carousel's claims map cleanly to the broader literature:
- Rename to PMOS: confirmed by the May 2026 Lancet consensus paper (Teede et al), endorsed by the International PCOS Network, the Society for Endocrinology, the Endocrine Society, and Mayo Clinic. The 3-year transition (full implementation by the 2028 International Guideline update) is in progress.
- Multisystem (hormones, metabolism, weight, insulin resistance, skin, fertility, periods, mental health): directly mirrors the 2023 Teede International Evidence-Based PCOS Guideline.
- Insulin resistance as a major driver: consistent with decades of literature (Dunaif 1989 onward); ~50–80% of women with PCOS/PMOS show insulin resistance independent of BMI.
- Lifestyle as first-line therapy: Grade A recommendation in the 2023 international guideline.
- Symptom list (irregular periods, acne, hirsutism, weight gain, subfertility): matches Rotterdam diagnostic features and is undisputed.

#### Pass 2 — SORT grade

**Grade A.** Consistent good-quality patient-oriented evidence supports the rename (consensus), the multisystem framing, the role of insulin resistance, and lifestyle modification as first-line. The post does not overreach into unproven territory; it stays within mainstream endocrinology.

The one place where I'd nudge toward Grade B is the unstated implication that NewME specifically is the right delivery vehicle for that lifestyle modification — that's a program-specific outcome claim with no public RCT-quality evidence to support it (which is true of essentially every commercial wellness program).
