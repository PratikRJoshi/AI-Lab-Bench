## Analysis

### Medical: SORT Analysis
The premise (many drugs were trialed for weeks–months but taken for years/decades, and chronic use accrues risks) is legitimate and increasingly emphasized in deprescribing literature. Each class is accurately characterized: PPIs (real associations with B12 deficiency at ≥2 yr ~+65% odds, hypomagnesemia, and kidney injury signals; mechanism via acid-dependent absorption) — though he correctly labels these observational; Z-drugs (GABA-receptor downregulation → dependence; FDA black-box for complex sleep behaviors — both factual); anticholinergics (a large meta-analysis links cumulative use to dementia in a dose-response manner; ~+46% at ≥3 months — and he properly raises reverse-causation/confounding); gabapentin (mostly off-label beyond its narrow approvals; cognitive/fall risks; physical dependence requiring taper). Crucially, he repeatedly carves out necessary indications (ulcers/H. pylori for PPIs; seizures for gabapentin) and insists on physician-guided tapering rather than abrupt discontinuation — responsible framing. The non-drug alternatives (GERD: visceral-fat loss, diaphragmatic breathing, post-meal walks; sleep hygiene; second-gen antihistamines; bladder/pelvic-floor training) are evidence-aligned.
