## Analysis

### SORT Analysis

#### Pass 1 — Four lenses

**Safety**
- The post itself poses no direct harm — it is a symptom awareness list.
- Risk: self-diagnosis based on a non-specific symptom list. All 9 symptoms have multiple other causes (hypothyroidism, depression, sleep deprivation, poor diet, insulin resistance, etc.). Presenting these as signs of "high cortisol" without differential diagnosis context could lead to unnecessary testing, self-medication, or misattribution.

**Outcomes**
Evaluating each claim against clinical evidence for hypercortisolism:

| Symptom | Clinically accurate for high cortisol? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bruising | ✅ Yes | Cushing's syndrome hallmark — cortisol thins skin |
| Puffy face | ✅ Yes | "Moon face" in Cushing's; also seen in hypothyroidism, fluid retention |
| Abdominal fat | ✅ Yes | Central obesity is a key Cushing's sign; also metabolic syndrome |
| Cravings for sugar and fat | ⚠️ Partially supported | Stress/cortisol increases appetite and hedonic eating; not a diagnostic sign |
| Low libido | ⚠️ Weakly specific | Many causes; cortisol suppresses gonadal hormones in chronic elevation |
| Irritability | ⚠️ Non-specific | Common with any HPA-axis dysregulation, sleep deprivation, thyroid issues |
| Neck tension | ⚠️ Very non-specific | Not a recognized clinical sign of hypercortisolism |
| Fatigue | ⚠️ Non-specific | Paradoxical: acute cortisol is energizing; fatigue can indicate adrenal fatigue or other causes |
| Acne | ⚠️ Weakly specific | Cortisol indirectly increases sebum; also caused by androgens, PCOS, diet |

**Risks of bias**
- No source cited. The list conflates Cushing's syndrome symptoms (a serious medical condition with pathological cortisol excess) with functional/lifestyle symptoms that may reflect chronic mild stress.
- "Cortisol" has become a wellness-industry buzzword — this type of content often leads to supplement sales for "cortisol blockers" without evidence.

**Total evidence**
- Clinical Cushing's syndrome (pathological hypercortisolism) has a well-established symptom profile: the first 3 listed symptoms (bruising, moon face, central obesity) are genuine markers. The remaining 6 are too non-specific to be diagnostically useful as cortisol indicators.
- "Cortisol dysregulation" as a subclinical condition is not a recognized medical diagnosis — it is wellness marketing language.

#### Pass 2 — SORT Grade

**Grade C** — Expert opinion / consensus at best. The 3 Cushing's-specific signs are evidence-backed for pathological hypercortisolism. The remaining 6 are non-specific and clinically misleading when framed as "signs of high cortisol."

## Verdict
The 3 classic Cushing's signs (bruising, moon face, central fat) are clinically accurate for pathological high cortisol. The other 6 signs are **too non-specific to be attributed to cortisol** without differential diagnosis. This content risks normalizing "cortisol as the root of all symptoms" — a popular wellness narrative that can delay proper diagnosis. If multiple symptoms apply, the appropriate action is a cortisol blood/urine test via a physician, not self-diagnosis. **Trustworthiness: Low-Moderate. SORT Grade C.**
