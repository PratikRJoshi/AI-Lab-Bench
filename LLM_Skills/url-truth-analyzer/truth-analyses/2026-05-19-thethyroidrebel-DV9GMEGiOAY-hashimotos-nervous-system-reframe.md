## Summary
A Hashimoto's-focused carousel that reframes thyroid autoimmunity as the body *protecting* rather than *attacking* itself, places trauma and chronic nervous-system dysregulation at the centre of autoimmune disease, and dismisses strict elimination diets/AIP and "toxin-avoidance routines" as actively counterproductive because they "scream threat" to the nervous system. The narrative closes with two paid offers — a "Putting Out the Thyroid Fire" workshop replay and a 16-week personalised programme called BLOOM — pitched as the path to remission.

## Channel Reputation

Aggie McCabe is a UK-based nutritional therapist (DipNT, mBANT, CNHC-registered) who writes regularly for Thyroid UK's member magazine and runs a Hashimoto's-focused practice combining functional-medicine nutrition with somatic / nervous-system work. Her content is consistently well-written and avoids the most flagrant anti-medicine talking points (she does not, for example, tell followers to drop levothyroxine), but it is structurally promotional — most narratives, including this one, funnel toward a paid programme. No notable fact-checker flags against her exist, but the commercial conflict of interest should be weighted into how recommendations land.

## Analysis

### SORT Analysis — Medical content

**Claims extracted from the carousel**

1. Hashimoto's autoimmunity is the body *protecting* you, not attacking you. *(Slides 1, 8, 13 — a metaphorical reframe, not a falsifiable medical claim.)*
2. Trauma is a "huge" root cause of, and result of, thyroid conditions. *(Slide 9.)*
3. The nervous system "marks everything as a threat" based on prediction and learnt behaviour, and the immune system is an "extension" of the nervous system. *(Slide 10.)*
4. Threat response → stress hormones + pro-inflammatory cytokines → autoimmune fire → more threat signalling → closed feedback loop driving chronic inflammation. *(Slide 11.)*
5. Strict elimination diets, toxin-vigilance, and rigid morning routines don't work long-term and may worsen autoimmunity by reinforcing threat signalling. *(Slide 12.)*
6. Retraining the nervous system, combined with broader "Thyroid Terrain" work, allows readers to "reach remission without the constant ebb and flow of symptoms, antibody spikes and dreading the next flare." *(Slide 14.)*
7. Call-to-action: comment "FIRE" for the workshop replay; comment "BLOOM" to enrol in the 16-week paid programme before spring enrolment closes. *(Slides 15–16.)*

#### Pass 1 — Four lenses

- **Safety.** Largely safe at the surface — no advice to drop thyroid meds, no extreme protocols, no dangerous supplements. The risks are softer: (a) telling Hashimoto's patients that lab-driven autoimmune workups and structured elimination diets are themselves "threat signals" could erode adherence to standard monitoring and care, and (b) selling a 16-week programme on a remission promise to a population that is often desperate is a meaningful financial-harm vector. Standard pathophysiology — TPO/Tg antibodies driving lymphocytic infiltration and follicular destruction — is well established and not reflected in the "protection" framing.
- **Outcomes (POEM vs DOE).** The promised outcome — *remission, fewer flares, no antibody spikes* — is a patient-oriented outcome (POEM). The evidence offered to support it is none: no trial data, no cohort, only mechanism + the creator's own story. The closest published patient-oriented data for somatic / mind-body work in Hashimoto's is a single small RCT (Markomanolaki 2019) showing modest QoL and thyroid-function improvements from an 8-week stress-management protocol — useful directional signal, not a remission story.
- **Risk of bias.** High. The author is the seller. Outcomes are uncontrolled testimonials. The carousel uses motivational rhetoric ("hopeful perspective", "softening, listening") rather than effect sizes or comparators. No conflict-of-interest disclosure beyond the implicit CTA.
- **Total evidence.** The stress / HPA-axis / autoimmunity link is real and supported by observational literature (Mizokami 2004, Vita 2009, Markomanolaki 2019). The Autoimmune Protocol critique is *partially* fair — the one published AIP-for-Hashimoto's pilot (Abbott 2019) found QoL gains but **no change in TPO antibodies or thyroid function**. The headline reframe ("body protecting you" → "retrain nervous system → remission"), and especially the dismissal of structured dietary/lifestyle interventions as net-harmful, runs ahead of the evidence.

#### Pass 2 — SORT Grade

**Grade C** overall.

- Claim 2 (stress/trauma ↔ Hashimoto's association): Grade **B** observational support exists; the carousel's "huge" framing exceeds it.
- Claims 1, 3, 4, 5, 6 (reframe, mechanism narrative, remission via nervous-system retraining, AIP-is-counterproductive): Grade **C** — mechanism-plausible, expert opinion, anecdote, no patient-oriented RCT support for the specific remission outcome being sold.

## Evidence / Validation Links
- Caturegli P, De Remigis A, Rose NR. *Hashimoto thyroiditis: clinical and diagnostic criteria.* Autoimmunity Reviews, 2014. — [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24434360/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24434360/) (standard pathophysiology — antibody-mediated follicular destruction, not "protection")
- Mizokami T, Wu Li A, El-Kaissi S, Wall JR. *Stress and thyroid autoimmunity.* Thyroid, 2004. — [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15650357/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15650357/) (canonical review of the stress / HPA-axis ↔ autoimmune thyroid link the carousel leans on)
- Markomanolaki ZS et al. *Stress Management in Women with Hashimoto's Thyroiditis: A Randomized Controlled Trial.* Journal of Molecular Biochemistry, 2019. — [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30881898/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30881898/) (the strongest interventional evidence in this space — modest QoL/thyroid-function gains; nowhere near "remission")
- Abbott RD, Sadowski A, Alt AG. *Efficacy of the Autoimmune Protocol Diet as Part of a Multi-Disciplinary, Supported Lifestyle Intervention for Hashimoto's Thyroiditis.* Cureus, 2019. — [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31058026/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31058026/) (the AIP pilot the carousel implicitly argues against — QoL up, antibodies unchanged)
- Mincer DL, Jialal I. *Hashimoto Thyroiditis.* StatPearls, 2024. — [https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459262/](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459262/) (current standard-of-care summary: levothyroxine replacement, periodic TSH monitoring, treat hypothyroidism, no established "remission protocol")

## Verdict
The carousel is emotionally well-pitched and lands on a real seam in the literature — chronic stress and autoimmune thyroid disease genuinely do interact, and the strict-AIP-as-cure narrative is overhyped in wellness culture. Where it overreaches is in the leap from *stress modulates autoimmunity* to *retraining the nervous system produces Hashimoto's remission*, a leap unsupported by any published RCT and used here as the pitch for a paid 16-week programme. Treat it as a compassionate reframe with a kernel of evidence and an unsupported sales promise on top; standard care (TSH monitoring, levothyroxine where indicated, plus stress management as adjunct) remains the floor.

## ELI5 — Friend to Friend
