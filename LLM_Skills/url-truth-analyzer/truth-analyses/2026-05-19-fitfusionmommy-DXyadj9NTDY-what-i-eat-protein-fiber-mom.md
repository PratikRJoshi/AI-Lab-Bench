## Analysis
### Medical: SORT Analysis

**Pass 1 — Four lenses**

- **Safety**: 120 g/day protein in a healthy adult woman (≈1.5–2.0 g/kg depending on body mass) is within the ISSN range (1.4–2.0 g/kg) for individuals doing resistance training and is safe in people without chronic kidney disease. 30 g/day fiber is at the upper end of US Dietary Guidelines for adult women (target ≈25–28 g) and well below intakes that cause GI distress in healthy gut. No red flags for the general postpartum population; lactating women actually have *higher* protein needs (~1.7–1.9 g/kg), so 120 g is, if anything, conservative for breastfeeding moms.
- **Outcomes (POEM vs DOE)**: The implied POEM is hypertrophy / body recomposition while caring for a toddler — a patient-relevant outcome. Supporting DOEs (muscle protein synthesis, nitrogen balance, fecal SCFA from fiber) are well-mapped to those POEMs in meta-analytic data, so the surrogate-to-outcome chain is reasonable.
- **Risk of Bias**: High individual-level bias — n = 1 anecdote, gym mirror-selfie framing, no disclosure of weight, training status, lactation, or kidney function. No commercial conflict declared on this specific reel, though the broader account often features protein-forward branded content.
- **Total Evidence**: Strong. The 1.6 g/kg/day protein figure is supported by a 49-study meta-analysis (Morton 2018, BJSM) showing diminishing returns above that threshold for resistance-trained adults. Fiber 25–30 g/day is endorsed by the 2020–2025 US Dietary Guidelines and the Reynolds 2019 Lancet meta-analysis (which actually found dose-response benefits up to ≈25–29 g/day for all-cause mortality, CHD, T2D, colorectal cancer).

**Pass 2 — SORT grade: B**

Grade B (inconsistent or limited-quality patient-oriented evidence at the individual prescription level). The *underlying* macros align with A-grade population evidence, but the post applies them as a one-size-fits-all daily target without weight-based scaling, lactation adjustment, or any caveat for individuals with CKD, IBS, or low baseline fiber tolerance. A registered dietitian giving the same numbers with individualization would warrant Grade A; presented as a generic Instagram prescription, it drops to B.

## Verdict
The headline numbers are not wrong — 120 g protein and 30 g fiber are defensible daily targets for a healthy adult woman doing resistance training, and they sit comfortably inside ISSN, ACSM, and US Dietary Guidelines recommendations. The problem is genre, not arithmetic: a 13-second reel can't substitute for individualized macro planning, and the creator has no surfaced clinical credentials. Followers in the "toddler mom building muscle" demographic who are otherwise healthy can use these numbers as a reasonable starting point; anyone with kidney disease, gut disorders, history of disordered eating, or atypical body weight should treat the figures as a content-creator anecdote, not a prescription.
