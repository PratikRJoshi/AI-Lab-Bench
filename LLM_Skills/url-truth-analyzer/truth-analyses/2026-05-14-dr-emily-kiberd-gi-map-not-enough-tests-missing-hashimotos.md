## Analysis

### SORT Analysis

**Claim**: Standard GI testing (including GI-MAP) and conventional gastroenterology miss important drivers of Hashimoto's symptoms (exhaustion, rising antibodies).

#### Safety Lens
- Discouraging trust in qualified gastroenterologists is potentially harmful.
- Antibody levels in Hashimoto's fluctuate naturally by 30-50% over months without any intervention — presenting "climbing antibodies" as proof of treatment failure is misleading.
- The supplement cabinet shown suggests polypharmacy with unregulated products. No discussion of interactions or monitoring.

#### Outcomes Lens
- The implicit claim is: her approach catches what standard testing misses, leading to symptom resolution. This is a POEM claim (symptom improvement) but supported only by testimonial, not by any comparative study.
- No specific alternative tests are named in this post — it's a cliffhanger designed to drive engagement/comments.
- TPO antibody levels (the "climbing antibodies") are DOEs, not POEMs. Antibody reduction doesn't reliably correlate with symptom improvement.

#### Risk of Bias Lens
- **Study design**: None. Pure clinical opinion in marketing format.
- **Conflict of interest**: Creator sells the alternative approach being promoted. The cabinet of expensive supplements (Thorne products retail $30-80 each; the cabinet shows 30+ bottles) represents significant ongoing cost to patients.
- **Framing bias**: "You've done everything right and it's still not working" is a powerful psychological hook that exploits frustrated patients. It positions her as the only remaining option.

#### Total Evidence Lens
- GI-MAP (Diagnostic Solutions Laboratory) is already a functional medicine test, not a "standard" gastroenterology tool. It uses qPCR to detect organisms. If even the functional medicine test isn't enough in her framework, what is?
- Conventional gastroenterology workup for chronic fatigue + autoimmune thyroiditis typically includes: celiac panel, H. pylori testing, inflammatory markers, iron studies, B12 — all well-validated.
- The "what tests aren't catching" gap she implies is likely: mycotoxin testing, organic acids testing, DUTCH hormone panels — all of which lack validation for Hashimoto's treatment guidance.

### SORT Grade: C
No evidence cited. Marketing content designed to undermine trust in qualified specialists and drive program enrollment.
