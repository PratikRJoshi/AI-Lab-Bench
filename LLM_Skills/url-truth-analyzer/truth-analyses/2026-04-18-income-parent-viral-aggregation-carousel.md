## Analysis

### Claim Validation

**Claim 1: "10 squats every 45 minutes = 10,000 steps" (Image 3 — @AyusWellness/Zib Atkins)**
**MISLEADING EQUIVALENCE — but the study is real.**

The "= 10,000 steps" framing is false. Steps and squats measure entirely different things:
- 10,000 steps = ~8 km of walking, ~400-500 calories, ~80-90 minutes of activity
- 10 squats every 45 min for 8 hours = ~110 total squats, negligible calorie expenditure, but high-intensity muscle recruitment

What the actual study (Scandinavian Journal of Medicine & Science in Sports, 2024) found: interrupting prolonged sitting with 10 squats every 45 minutes reduced post-meal blood glucose spikes by **21%** — approximately **double** the benefit of a single 30-minute walk. This is because squats maximally recruit the quadriceps, glutes, and hamstrings (major glucose-consuming muscles), generating lactate that facilitates glucose transport into muscle cells for up to 48 hours.

**The study attribution is wrong** — this is NOT a University of Texas study. The 2024 study was from Australian/UK institutions (not specified in the post). There is a related earlier study (Dempsey et al., 2016, *Diabetes Care*) from the University of South Australia. The "University of Texas" attribution appears fabricated.

**Claim 2: "10 squats every hour control blood sugar better than a 30-minute walk" (Images 3-4)**
**SUPPORTED by actual published research.** The 2024 study confirmed this specific finding for postprandial blood glucose in overweight/obese men. However:
- Study population: 18 overweight/obese men only — narrow generalizability
- Outcome measured: postprandial glucose spike specifically, not long-term glycemic control or HbA1c
- The mechanism (image 4) — legs pulling glucose out of bloodstream, improving circulation — is directionally accurate
- This is consistent with broader "exercise snacking" literature showing brief frequent exercise bouts are effective for glycemic management

**Claim 3: "With one dose of Ibogaine, more than 80% of people are free of that addiction. With two doses, it's more than 90%" — Joe Rogan quote (Image 9)**
**UNSUBSTANTIATED at this specific figure.**

Ibogaine (from Tabernanthe iboga plant) does have real research for addiction treatment, particularly opioid addiction. Real findings from published literature:
- Small observational studies (e.g., Noller et al., 2018, Am J Drug Alcohol Abuse) show significant opioid use reduction after ibogaine treatment
- The existing evidence is from small, uncontrolled studies (10-100 patients) in specialized clinics
- No RCT has been published establishing 80-90% success rates
- Ibogaine has serious cardiac risks (QT prolongation → fatal arrhythmia), with multiple documented deaths
- FDA has not approved ibogaine; its Schedule I status in the US makes clinical research difficult

The "80%/90% free of addiction" figures Joe Rogan cites are not from published peer-reviewed literature — they appear to be anecdotal or from clinics' own promotional data. While ibogaine shows real promise, these specific figures are not established science.

## Verdict

The squat/blood sugar content is real — there is published research showing that brief squat breaks every 45 minutes reduce postprandial glucose spikes better than a single 30-minute walk. The University of Texas attribution is wrong, and "= 10,000 steps" is a false equivalence, but the core recommendation (do 10 squats every 45-60 minutes if you sit all day) is evidence-based. The ibogaine "80-90%" claim is unsubstantiated by peer-reviewed data and ignores serious cardiac safety concerns. The remaining 8 slides are viral financial/political content entirely unrelated to health.
