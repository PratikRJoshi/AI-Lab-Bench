## Summary
Dr. Jess Steier (@unbiasedscipod, DrPH in Health Policy) partners with Spotify's *Science Vs* to ask whether running is a "miracle exercise or a joint killer." Cover slide cites >28 million weekly runners in the US. The caption's core theses: (a) running does NOT ruin healthy knees; (b) most "running injuries" trace to training errors, not the activity itself; (c) even small doses of running confer meaningful mortality/cardiovascular benefit.

## Analysis

### SORT Analysis

**Claim 1 — "Running doesn't ruin your knees."**
Evidence: Recreational running is associated with **lower**, not higher, rates of knee and hip osteoarthritis. Alentorn-Geli et al. AJSM 2017 meta-analysis (17 studies, 114,829 subjects): recreational runners 3.5% OA prevalence vs. 10.2% sedentary and 13.3% competitive/elite runners — U-shaped dose response. Timmins et al. Arthritis Care Res 2017 reached the same conclusion.
**SORT: A** — consistent POEMs from meta-analyzed cohorts.

**Claim 2 — "Most running injuries are training errors, not running itself."**
Evidence: Nielsen et al. BJSM 2014 and systematic reviews (van Gent BJSM 2007, van der Worp PLoS ONE 2015) identify rapid weekly mileage increases, inadequate recovery, and previous injury as dominant risk factors; biomechanics and surface matter far less than training load. The "10% rule" is itself not well RCT-validated (Buist AJSM 2008 couldn't confirm it), but the broader "ramp rate matters more than total volume" principle is robust.
**SORT: B** — consistent in direction, variable in magnitude; most evidence is observational with methodological heterogeneity.

**Claim 3 — "Even small amounts of running are linked to meaningful health benefits."**
Evidence: Lee et al. JACC 2014 landmark cohort (55,137 adults, 15 yr follow-up): runners had **30% lower all-cause mortality** and **45% lower CV mortality** vs. non-runners. Benefit appeared at <51 min/week, <10 km/week, 1–2 sessions/week, <6 mph. Pedisic BJSM 2020 meta-analysis (14 studies, 232,149 participants) confirmed: any amount of running lowers all-cause (27%), CV (30%), and cancer (23%) mortality. No additional benefit beyond a threshold, but no harm from more.
**SORT: A** — consistent POEMs from large prospective cohorts and meta-analyses.

### Pass 2 — Overall Grade
**A–** overall. Claims 1 and 3 are Grade A; Claim 2 is Grade B (directionally right, quantitatively fuzzy). The post editorially hedges appropriately ("the story was a lot more nuanced"), and the source is a DrPH-led podcast + Spotify's Science Vs (which employs a staff research team + peer-reviewed citations). No supplement sold, no product affiliate, no clickbait promise of "fix your knees in 30 days."

## Verdict
Good-faith science communication by credentialed hosts pointing to peer-reviewed primary sources. The three headline claims all survive scrutiny. The one caveat is that the post (via its "small amounts have meaningful benefit" framing) understates the mild **U-shape** at very high volumes — elite runners have slightly more knee-OA risk than recreational — but for ~99% of readers, "running is good for you, train gradually, injuries aren't inherent to the activity" is the correct take.

## ELI5 — Friend to Friend
Legit. Running the normal amount a hobby runner does (a few miles a few times a week) lowers your risk of heart disease and dying early, and does **not** wreck your knees — actually the opposite; it's sitting on the couch that's worse for cartilage. Most injuries come from ramping mileage too fast, not from running itself. So if you've been avoiding running because "it's bad for your joints," the data says you've been scared off by a myth.
