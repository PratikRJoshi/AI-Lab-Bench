## Analysis

### Medical: SORT Analysis
The exercise claims are the strongest and best-evidenced (large meta-analyses: exercise preferentially reduces visceral fat; vigorous aerobic/HIIT most effective; dose-dependent). The food claims (carotenoids, green-tea catechins) rest on real RCTs but with modest effect sizes and largely Japanese cohorts — Dr. Kim appropriately frames them as accelerants on top of a calorie deficit rather than magic. The "scale is a poor indicator of body-composition change" point is accurate and clinically responsible. The single cited "48% visceral fat reduction from 2×45-min cycling/week" is an older small study presented with honest hedging about VO2peak interpretation. No fat-spot-reduction overreach; he correctly says diet drives total weight while exercise drives visceral loss.

## Verdict
Strongest: C3/C4 (exercise reduces visceral fat, vigorous aerobic/HIIT best, dose-dependent) — backed by large meta-analytic evidence and presented accurately. Weakest: C2 (carotenoids) — a single modest Japanese RCT, fairly labeled "Contested." The green-tea claim (C1) is verified via Nagao 2007 but the real-world effect is small. Overall responsible, non-sensational, accurate; recommend sharing. Note citations [2],[3] carry `[UNVERIFIED]` status (well-established but not each re-fetched); [1] is verified.
