## Analysis

### Science: Claim Validation
Anxious attachment is operationally defined by elevated abandonment anxiety and hypervigilance to rejection cues — exactly the "fear of abandonment in small moments" described [1]. The developmental framing ("built somewhere it made sense") aligns with attachment theory's view that internal working models form from early caregiving environments [1]. The advice — regulate your own activation rather than fully outsourcing it to the partner — matches the evidence that anxious hyperactivating strategies (excessive reassurance-seeking) can strain relationships, while self-plus-co-regulation is healthier [1][2]. Responsible, non-pathologizing framing. No overreach.

## ELI5 — Friend to Friend
If you're the anxious one, that gnawing "they're going to leave" feeling is real and it came from somewhere that made sense — you're not broken. The growth move isn't to stop feeling it; it's to soothe yourself a bit before dumping 100% of it on your partner. This post nails that. Try one self-calming step (a walk, a breath, a journal line) before asking for reassurance next time.
