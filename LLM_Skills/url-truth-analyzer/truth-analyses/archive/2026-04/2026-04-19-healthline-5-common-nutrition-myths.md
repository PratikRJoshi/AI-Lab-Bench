## Analysis

### SORT Analysis (Myth #1 — Eggs and Heart Disease)

**Lens 1 — Safety**: The claim is "in healthy individuals." This hedge is important: ~25% of people are "hyperresponders" whose LDL rises meaningfully with dietary cholesterol (Fernandez 2012, Vincent et al. 2019); the slide glosses this subpopulation. For people with familial hypercholesterolemia, diabetes, or existing CVD, the egg-intake signal is less clean (Zhong et al. JAMA 2019 showed a dose-response risk in pooled U.S. cohorts). Post correctly restricts to "most/healthy" but a reader with metabolic disease may miss this.

**Lens 2 — Outcomes**: Claim uses patient-oriented outcomes (heart disease, not just LDL). This is the correct framing — egg studies powered for cardiovascular mortality (Drouin-Chartier et al. BMJ 2020, >210k participants, 3M+ person-years) find ≤1 egg/day not associated with CVD incidence in generally healthy Western and Asian cohorts.

**Lens 3 — Risks of bias**: Source is a registered dietitian-reviewed article published by Healthline (owned by Red Ventures; ad-supported but editorially independent). Original primary evidence cited in the Healthline article includes RCTs on cholesterol response (Fernandez 2006) and large prospective cohorts (BMJ 2013, PMID 23295181). No egg-industry funding disclosed on the piece. Study-design quality is a mix of RCTs (small, short) + large cohorts (confounding possible) — typical nutritional-epidemiology stack.

**Lens 4 — Total evidence**: Consistent with (a) 2015 Dietary Guidelines for Americans removing the 300 mg/day cholesterol cap, (b) AHA 2019 Science Advisory allowing up to 1 egg/day for healthy adults and up to 2 for vegetarians, (c) 2020 BMJ meta-analysis (Drouin-Chartier), (d) 2023 Nutrients umbrella review. One notable outlier: Zhong 2019 JAMA pooled analysis did find dose-dependent CVD association, which tempers but does not overturn the consensus.

### SORT Grade — **B**
Patient-oriented, but evidence is inconsistent at the margins (hyperresponders, diabetics, Zhong 2019 contradiction). Grade A would require fully consistent POEMs; the field isn't there. The post's framing is accurate for the majority population but oversimplifies the minority signal.

## Verdict
Solid evidence-based repackage from a mainstream medical-reviewed publisher. The lead (and only fully recoverable) myth-bust — eggs don't raise heart disease risk in healthy people — reflects current major-society consensus, though it slightly under-represents the hyperresponder and diabetic subgroups. Because the source article was reviewed by an RD and the post by a second RD, and because the caption hedges with "for most people" / "healthy individuals," the editorial framing is careful. Comfortable to share with a curious friend.

## ELI5 — Friend to Friend
Yep, this one's legit. An egg a day isn't going to wreck your heart if you're otherwise healthy — that's what big reviews of hundreds of thousands of people actually show, and it's why official US dietary guidelines dropped the old "300 mg cholesterol cap" back in 2015. Just note: "for most people" is doing real work in that sentence — if you have diabetes or high cholesterol that runs in your family, still loop in your doctor before you go full 6-egg-omelette life.
