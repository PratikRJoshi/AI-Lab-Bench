## Summary
A 17-slide carousel from @thegutgarage arguing that the root cause of fiber-induced bloating is pre-existing colon stasis ("backed-up colon"), not fiber sensitivity. The protocol proposed is: first restore motility (hydration with warm salt water, magnesium citrate, movement/twists), then reintroduce fiber slowly. The post frames this as a two-step sequence: "start with flow, end with fiber." It warns that adding fiber to a "clogged" colon amplifies gas and pressure rather than helping.

## Analysis

### SORT Analysis

#### Pass 1 — Four lenses

**Safety**
- The hydration recommendation (2–3 cups warm water + pinch of sea salt/trace minerals on waking) is safe for most people and consistent with general bowel hygiene practices.
- Magnesium citrate/sulfate recommendation includes an appropriate caveat: "with your doctor's guidance." This is important — magnesium citrate at laxative doses can be unsafe in kidney disease.
- No dangerous supplements or extreme protocols suggested.
- The advice to start fiber low and slow (1 tsp psyllium/flax/chia) is conservative and appropriate.

**Outcomes**
Evaluating key claims:

**Claim 1: Bloating after fiber usually means the colon is already "backed up" with slow-moving stool, not fiber sensitivity.**
- **Status**: ✅ Supported — directionally accurate. This is a well-recognized clinical phenomenon. Slow transit constipation (STC) causes fermentation of newly ingested fiber before it can pass, producing gas and distension. Gastroenterology practice commonly observes that fiber can worsen symptoms in constipated patients if motility is not first addressed.
- Caveat: The claim that this is the *usual* cause (vs. SIBO, IBS-D, dysbiosis, food intolerances) is an oversimplification. Fiber bloating has multiple etiologies.

**Claim 2: "You can poop every day and still be constipated" (incomplete evacuation).**
- **Status**: ✅ Well-supported. Outlet obstruction, pelvic floor dysfunction, and dyssynergic defecation are recognized causes of incomplete evacuation even with daily bowel movements. This is clinically accurate.

**Claim 3: Warm water + sea salt triggers the gastrocolic reflex.**
- **Status**: ⚠️ Plausible but oversimplified. Warm liquid in the stomach does stimulate the gastrocolic reflex via vagal signaling. The sea salt addition is not evidence-based as a specific enhancer — water temperature is the active ingredient. Harmless but not proven.

**Claim 4: Fiber produces butyrate that heals the colon lining.**
- **Status**: ✅ Well-supported. Short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), especially butyrate produced by fermentation of soluble fiber by colonic bacteria, are the primary energy source for colonocytes and have well-established anti-inflammatory and epithelial barrier-supporting roles.

**Claim 5: Vagus nerve controls both mood and motility; tense brain = tense colon.**
- **Status**: ✅ Supported. The brain-gut axis mediated by the vagus nerve is extensively studied. Autonomic dysregulation (sympathetic dominance) does reduce gut motility and increase visceral hypersensitivity. This is mainstream gastroenterology.

**Claim 6: Kiwi, prunes, chia as "natural stool gliders."**
- **Status**: ✅ Supported. Kiwifruit has level 1 evidence for improving stool frequency and consistency (Ibarra et al., 2022, *American Journal of Gastroenterology*). Prunes are well-documented for mild constipation.

**Risks of bias**
- The post is a product funnel — it concludes with "Comment RESET for the link + 50% off" for "The Gut Reset Blueprint." The advice is medically reasonable but clearly designed to drive product sales.
- No citations are provided. Content is presented as authoritative without sourcing.
- The account name (@thegutgarage) and disclaimer ("not medical advice") signal commercial intent.

**Total evidence**
- The core protocol (motility before fiber) is consistent with clinical gastroenterology practice and the evidence base for irritable bowel syndrome and functional constipation management.
- This is essentially a simplified version of the "low-FODMAP restart" or functional constipation management approach, described accessibly but without nuance about when to see a doctor.

#### Pass 2 — SORT Grade

**Grade B** — Inconsistent/limited patient-oriented evidence. The individual components (fiber + motility sequencing, butyrate, vagal tone, kiwi) are supported by evidence of varying quality. The specific protocol presented as a 2-step sequence lacks direct RCT validation as a combined intervention.

## Verdict
The core claim — that fiber worsens bloating when the colon lacks motility, and that restoring flow before adding fiber is the correct sequence — is **clinically sound and consistent with gastroenterology practice**. The individual recommendations (warm water, magnesium with guidance, low-and-slow fiber, movement) are safe and reasonable. The post is promotional (product funnel), lacks citations, and oversimplifies the differential diagnosis of fiber bloating. Nevertheless, the medical content is more accurate than typical wellness social media. **Trustworthiness: Moderate-High for the core protocol. SORT Grade B.**
