## Analysis

### SORT Analysis

#### Pass 1 — Four Lenses

**Safety**: Recommending GI-MAP testing (26% specificity, 75% false positive rate, not FDA-cleared) could lead to unnecessary antimicrobial treatments based on false positives. No mention of continuing levothyroxine or working with an endocrinologist. The framing implicitly positions her coaching as an alternative to standard care.

**Outcomes**: All outcomes are DOEs at best (TPO antibodies, gut markers). Zero POEMs (remission rates, symptom scores, quality of life). The "restored gut balance reduced antibodies" claim cites no specific study with sample size, effect size, or follow-up period.

**Risk of Bias**: Commercial interest is explicit — every slide ends with a CTA for paid coaching. DC credentials are not equivalent to endocrinology training. "Reversed my own Hashimoto's" is unverifiable anecdote. No citations on any slide.

**Total Evidence**: The gut-thyroid axis is real (Cayres 2024, Knezevic 2020 — established in prior analyses). Mycotoxin-immune dysregulation is real but poorly characterized for Hashimoto's specifically. However, the leap from "these associations exist" to "this is your root cause and I can fix it" is entirely unsupported by clinical trial data.

#### Pass 2 — Claim-by-Claim

| Claim | Verdict | Grade |
|-------|---------|-------|

**Overall SORT Grade: C** — Mechanistic reasoning with kernels of truth, wrapped in unsupported diagnostic and therapeutic claims, from a chiropractor selling coaching. Zero citations, zero clinical outcome data.

## Verdict
The post layers real immunology concepts (gut-immune axis, mycotoxin effects, parasitic infections) onto an unsupported "root cause" narrative designed to sell coaching. The most concerning recommendation is the GI-MAP test, which has a 75% false positive rate for pathogen detection — meaning 3 out of 4 times it flags something that isn't there, potentially leading to unnecessary antimicrobial treatment. Standard Hashimoto's care (levothyroxine + TSH/fT4 monitoring) is backed by decades of Grade A evidence. Nothing in this post provides a reason to replace it.
