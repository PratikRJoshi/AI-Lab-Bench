## Analysis

### SORT Analysis

| Claim | Verdict | Grade |
|-------|---------|-------|
| Resistance training benefits Hashimoto's patients | **Supported** — multiple studies show RT improves body composition, fatigue, and quality of life in hypothyroid patients | B |
| RT supports "thyroid hormone turnover" | **Partially supported** — exercise increases T3/T4 metabolism; the phrase "turnover" is mechanistically vague | B-C |
| Advice to "take it easy" is a "massive disservice" | **Overstated** — some patients DO need to moderate exercise intensity during flares; blanket dismissal is irresponsible | C |

**Overall SORT Grade: B-C** — The core exercise recommendation is the most evidence-supported content from this account. However, the absolutist framing ("non-negotiable") ignores individual variation, disease severity, and the legitimate reasons some practitioners recommend moderation.

## Evidence / Validation Links
1. Lankhaar, J.A., et al. "Impact of overt and subclinical hypothyroidism on exercise tolerance." *Thyroid Research*, 2014.
2. Werneck, F.Z., et al. "Exercise training improves quality of life in women with subclinical hypothyroidism." *Arch Endocrinol Metab*, 2018.

## Verdict
Best post from this account — resistance training genuinely helps Hashimoto's patients. But the absolutist "non-negotiable" framing dismisses legitimate reasons for exercise modification, and the "free guide" is a coaching sales funnel.

## ELI5 — Friend to Friend
Actually decent advice for once — lifting weights IS good for thyroid patients. But "non-negotiable" is too strong. Some people with Hashimoto's genuinely need to start slow, especially during flares. The free guide is a gateway to paid coaching. Take the advice, skip the funnel.
