## Analysis

### Science / EBM
The science claims are well-supported:
- **Women have 70–80% lower endogenous creatine stores**: a *Nutrients* (2021) women's-health review states "females exhibiting 70–80% lower endogenous creatine stores compared to males" [3] — the exact figure she cites. ✔
- **Safety up to 30 g/day for 5 years (ISSN)**: ISSN position stand: "no compelling scientific evidence that the short- or long-term use of creatine monohydrate (up to 30 g/day for 5 years) has any detrimental effects on otherwise healthy individuals" [1]. ✔ exact match.
- **Most-studied / 500+ studies**: ISSN describes creatine monohydrate as among the most extensively studied and safe ergogenic aids; "500+ studies" is a commonly cited and reasonable order-of-magnitude figure [1][2].
- **3–5 g/day, no loading needed**: standard maintenance dose is 3–5 g/day; loading (20 g/day for 5–7 days) is optional, not required [2][4]. ✔
- **Brain/mood/cognition benefits**: supplementation raises brain creatine ~5–15%, can reduce mental fatigue and blunt cognitive decrement after sleep deprivation; women's-health review notes mood/depression benefits, possibly greater in females [1][3]. ✔
- **Healthy kidneys / hydration**: safe in those with healthy kidneys; hydration advice is sensible (creatine draws water into muscle). ✔

The only soft spots are commercial, not scientific: "most affordable" and the specific MuscleBlaze product are marketing, and "you're missing out if you skip it" overstates necessity (creatine is beneficial, not mandatory).
