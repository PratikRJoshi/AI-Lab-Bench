## Summary
Probiotic supplements are largely marketing over evidence. ## Analysis

### Science / EBM
This tracks the 2020 **American Gastroenterological Association (AGA)** clinical guideline almost point-for-point [1][2]:
- **Weak evidence for healthy people / most conditions**: AGA found insufficient evidence to recommend probiotics for most digestive conditions; it explicitly suggested patients with Crohn's, UC, IBS, or C. diff *consider stopping* given cost and lack of proven benefit [1][2]. ✔ matches "evidence is weak as hell" and "mostly hype."
- **Strain-specific, not universal**: AGA stresses effects are "not species-specific, but strain- and combination-specific" [1]. ✔ matches "different strains in different bottles, results all over the place."
- **Transient colonization**: Many strains don't colonize and are gone from stool 1–4 weeks after stopping; effects are transient and require continued consumption [3][4]. ✔ matches "they don't stay... if you stop, your gut won't notice."
- **The real exception**: AGA recommends specific probiotic formulations to **prevent C. difficile infection in adults/children taking antibiotics** (and NEC in preterm infants, pouchitis) [1][2]. ✔ matches her "prescribed with an antibiotic course to prevent diarrhea, not as a daily vitamin."
- **Food-first advice** (fiber, fermented foods, plant diversity to feed your own microbiome) is sound and consistent with mainstream nutrition guidance.

This is an unusually accurate consumer-health message.
