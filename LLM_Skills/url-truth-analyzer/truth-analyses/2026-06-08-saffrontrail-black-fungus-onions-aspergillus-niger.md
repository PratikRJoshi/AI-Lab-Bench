## Summary
The black sooty coating increasingly seen on onions is *Aspergillus niger*, a fungus that thrives in warm, humid, poorly-ventilated storage (worsened by quick-commerce warehousing). If it's only on the outer papery skin: peel, wash, cook. If dark streaks are inside the fleshy layers: keep peeling to clean pink-white flesh. If musty-smelling or slimy: discard. Some strains produce mycotoxins (she cites nausea, rarely liver issues). Wash hands/chopping board after; store in mesh (not plastic); buy fresh and in person.

## Analysis

### Science / EBM
The mycology and food-safety guidance are correct and align with agricultural-extension and clinical sources. The black coating is indeed *Aspergillus niger* ("black mold of onions"), ubiquitous in soil/air, developing under hot/humid conditions [1][2]. It's "harmless for the most part" when limited to outer skins — peel and wash — but should be discarded if growth penetrates or the onion is soft/slimy/musty [2][3]. The mycotoxin caution is legitimate: certain *A. niger* strains can produce **ochratoxin A**, a heat-resistant toxin associated with kidney (and to a lesser degree liver) harm with long-term exposure [3]. Immunocompromised people (and those with uncontrolled diabetes or chronic liver/lung disease) should avoid moldy produce entirely [3]. The storage/hygiene tips (mesh not plastic, wash board/hands, spores can spread) are sound. Minor nuance: she emphasizes "nausea and rarely liver issues," whereas the better-documented chronic concern is the kidney; and *A. niger* is generally lower-risk than relatives like *A. fumigatus* (respiratory) — but her conservative "when in doubt, throw it out" advice is exactly right.

## ELI5 — Friend to Friend
That black soot on your onions isn't dirt — it's a mold called Aspergillus niger, and it's everywhere now thanks to humid warehouse storage. The rule is simple: if it's just on the dry skin, peel/wash/cook and you're fine; if it's streaked inside or the onion's slimy/musty, bin it. A few strains make a toxin (mostly a long-term kidney worry), so don't push your luck. Solid, doctor-grade advice.
