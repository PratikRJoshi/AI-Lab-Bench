## EBM SORT analysis

### C1 — "Mineral sunscreen is not damaging to skin"
- **Verdict:** Partly true but oversimplified. **SORT B** (consistent, lower-quality patient-oriented evidence).
- Mineral filters (zinc oxide, titanium dioxide) are GRASE (Generally Recognized As Safe and Effective) under the FDA's 2021 sunscreen monograph proposal — the only two filters affirmatively classified GRASE. They sit on the skin surface, have minimal systemic absorption, and are well tolerated in sensitive skin.
- However, "not damaging" overstates it: nano-formulations, fragrance, and other excipients can still irritate. Efficacy depends on application thickness and reapplication, not the filter class alone.

### C2 — "Petroleum-based / chemical sunscreens are bad, never touch them"
- **Verdict:** Misleading / **SORT C recommendation against** the influencer's blanket warning.
- "Petroleum based" is a colloquial misnomer — chemical UV filters (avobenzone, octocrylene, oxybenzone, octisalate, homosalate, octinoxate) are organic small molecules, not petroleum jelly.
- The FDA in 2019–2020 found measurable systemic absorption of several filters above the 0.5 ng/mL threshold that triggers further toxicology data requests (Matta et al., JAMA 2019; 2020). Absorption ≠ harm; the FDA explicitly stated consumers should continue using sunscreen while data are gathered.
- The American Academy of Dermatology's position is unchanged: **the best sunscreen is the one you will use**; broad-spectrum SPF 30+, water-resistant, applied adequately. AAD does not advise avoiding chemical filters for the general adult population.
- Real-world risk of UV-induced skin cancer and photoaging is well-established; switching from a chemical sunscreen the user tolerates to no sunscreen because of fear-based content is the more dangerous outcome.

### C3 — "Water cream is perfect for summer"
- **Verdict:** Reasonable preference, not a health claim. **SORT C** (expert opinion / consumer preference).
- Lighter, water-based moisturizers (humectant-forward, low occlusive) are commonly recommended for humid weather and oily/combination skin to reduce comedogenicity and tackiness. This is texture/comfort, not safety.

### C4 — "Reusable cloths > disposable wipes"
- **Verdict:** Plausible but conditional. **SORT C**.
- Disposable cleansing wipes often leave surfactant residue and may contain preservatives (e.g., methylisothiazolinone historically) that can sensitize skin. Mechanical wiping can be irritating regardless of substrate.
- Reusable microfiber cloths are only better **if laundered hot and frequently** — used cloths can harbor *S. aureus* and *Pseudomonas* and worsen acne or periorbital irritation. The reel does not mention hygiene.

## Bottom line
The reel is a stylistic "non-toxic vs toxic" binary with one defensible call (mineral filters are well-tolerated), one misleading scare (chemical/"petroleum-based" sunscreen avoidance), and two preference claims dressed as health rules. A nutritionist credential does not extend to dermatologic or sunscreen pharmacology, and the influencer offers no citations.

**Take action:** keep using a sunscreen you will actually apply; if you prefer mineral for tolerability or environmental reasons, that's reasonable, but do not abandon chemical SPF over Instagram fear-marketing.

## Citations (from training, not web-fetched)
1. **U.S. FDA, 2021** — Proposed sunscreen monograph (final order under CARES Act). Zinc oxide and titanium dioxide are the only filters affirmatively classified GRASE I; chemical filters require additional safety data but are **not banned** and remain marketable.
2. **Matta MK et al., JAMA. 2019;321(21):2082–2091; JAMA. 2020;323(3):256–267.** — Demonstrated systemic absorption of avobenzone, oxybenzone, octocrylene, ecamsule above 0.5 ng/mL; FDA explicitly stated absorption alone does not indicate risk and recommended continued sunscreen use.
3. **American Academy of Dermatology Association** — Standing consumer guidance: broad-spectrum SPF 30+, water-resistant; mineral or chemical both acceptable; "the best sunscreen is the one you will use."
