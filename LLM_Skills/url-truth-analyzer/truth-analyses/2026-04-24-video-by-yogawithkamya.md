## Summary
This Instagram reel by yoga instructor Kamya claims that periods will become regular within 30 days if the viewer performs a set of yoga asanas daily — specifically forward fold (Uttanasana), Malasana (garland pose), and Supta Baddha Konasana (reclined butterfly pose) with coordinated breathing. ## Analysis

### SORT Analysis (Strength Of Recommendation Taxonomy)

#### Claims Identified
1. **Claim 1**: Periods will become regular within 30 days by performing these yoga asanas daily.
2. **Claim 2**: These specific poses (forward fold, Malasana, butterfly) will normalize menstrual flow.
3. **Claim 3**: Practicing these poses will reduce period pain (dysmenorrhea).

---

#### Lens 1 — Safety
- **No contraindications mentioned**: The reel does not warn about conditions where these poses may be harmful (pregnancy, severe endometriosis, uterine fibroids, recent surgery, joint injuries).
- **No guidance on when to see a doctor**: Irregular periods can be a symptom of serious conditions — PCOS, thyroid disorders, pituitary tumors, premature ovarian failure — that require medical evaluation. The reel implies yoga alone is sufficient.
- **No absolute risk numbers**: No quantification of how many people this helps vs. does not. No mention of harms.
- **Malasana caution omitted**: Deep squats like Malasana are contraindicated for people with knee injuries or hip joint issues — not mentioned.

#### Lens 2 — Outcomes (POEMs vs DOEs)
- All three claims target patient-oriented outcomes (menstrual regularity, flow normalization, pain reduction) — these are genuine POEMs.
- However, **no evidence is cited** to support any of these outcomes. The claims are presented as guaranteed results ("periods WILL become regular") rather than possibilities.
- The strongest existing evidence supports yoga for **dysmenorrhea (pain relief)** — Claim 3 — but even that evidence comes from comprehensive yoga programs, not isolated poses.
- Claims 1 and 2 (cycle regulation and flow normalization) have much weaker evidence support, particularly within a 30-day timeframe.

#### Lens 3 — Risk of Bias
- **No studies cited**: The reel provides zero references to clinical research.
- **Study design level**: This amounts to **expert opinion / anecdote** (Level 7 on the evidence hierarchy). The creator shares personal practice recommendations without clinical evidence.
- **Creator credentials**: Kamya is identified as a yoga teacher who discovered yoga during the pandemic while dealing with health issues. No formal medical or clinical training is documented. No yoga therapy certification (e.g., C-IAYT) has been identified.
- **Selection bias**: As a yoga content creator, there is inherent bias toward presenting yoga as a solution.
- **No control group or comparison**: No mention of what happens without yoga, or how yoga compares to medical treatment.
- **Definitive language**: The phrase "periods WILL become regular in 30 days" implies a guaranteed outcome, which is misleading.

#### Lens 4 — Total Evidence
- **What the research actually shows**:
  - A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis of 14 RCTs found yoga can reduce PMS symptoms including menstrual pain and distress (McGovern & Cheung, 2024).
  - A 2017 systematic review of 15 studies found reduced menstrual distress symptoms following yoga, but noted significant heterogeneity that limits generalizability (McGovern & Cheung, 2017).
  - A meta-analysis of 4 RCTs (230 participants) found yoga had a large effect on menstrual pain in primary dysmenorrhea (Oates, 2017).
  - For PCOS-related menstrual irregularity, a 2023 meta-analysis found yoga MAY help, but rated the evidence as "low quality" (Defined et al., 2023).
- **Critical gaps in the reel's claims**:
  - Most studies used **6–12 week** intervention periods, not 30 days. No study supports a 30-day timeline for menstrual regulation.
  - Studies used **comprehensive yoga programs** (multiple poses, pranayama, meditation, yoga nidra) — not just 3 isolated poses.
  - Evidence is strongest for **pain relief** (Claim 3) and weakest for **cycle regulation** (Claim 1).
  - Yoga is studied as an **adjunct** to medical care, not a replacement.

---

### SORT Grade: C

**Rationale**: The specific claims in this reel — that these three poses will regulate periods within 30 days — are supported only by anecdotal/expert opinion-level evidence. While the broader concept of "yoga may help with menstrual symptoms" has limited RCT support (Grade B level), the reel makes specific, guaranteed, time-bound claims that no clinical study supports. The evidence base consists of small, heterogeneous studies with methodological limitations, and the 30-day timeline is entirely unsupported. The grade reflects the content as presented, not the general topic area.

## Verdict
This reel overpromises and under-delivers on evidence. While yoga has emerging evidence as a complementary approach for menstrual pain relief, the specific claim that "periods will become regular in 30 days" from just three yoga poses is not supported by any clinical trial. The existing research uses longer intervention periods (6–12 weeks), comprehensive yoga programs (not 3 isolated poses), and typically studies yoga as an add-on to medical care — not a standalone cure. The reel omits important contraindications, fails to mention that irregular periods can signal serious medical conditions requiring evaluation, and uses language implying guaranteed results. The creator, while popular, does not have documented clinical or medical credentials. Grade C reflects that the claims rest on anecdote and expert opinion rather than rigorous evidence.
