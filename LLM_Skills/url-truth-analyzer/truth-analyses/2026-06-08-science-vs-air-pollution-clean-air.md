## Analysis

### Science / EBM
The core claim is consensus science. Particulate matter (PM2.5) is "an important modifiable risk factor for significantly increased morbidity and mortality," with both acute and chronic exposure raising risk of ischemic heart disease, heart failure, and stroke, and acting as an endocrine disruptor contributing to obesity/diabetes [2]. PM2.5 is small enough to lodge in the lungs and pass into the bloodstream and organs [1]. Maternal PM2.5 exposure is linked to adverse birth outcomes (low birth weight, developmental delays) via inflammatory pathways affecting both mother and fetus [3]. The Ella Adoo-Kissi-Debrah case is real and landmark: after a 2020 inquest she became the first person to have air pollution listed as a cause of death on a death certificate [1]. The one soft spot is "increase your risk of going blind" — there is emerging evidence linking air pollution to eye diseases (AMD/glaucoma), but it's far less established than the cardiovascular/respiratory/fetal effects, so that specific line outruns the strongest evidence.

## Verdict
The central message (**C1–C3**) is robust mainstream science — PM2.5 is a major driver of cardiovascular, respiratory, and fetal harm, and air pollution has even been formally recorded as a cause of death [1][2][3]. Only **C4** ("going blind") outruns the strongest evidence and should be treated as emerging, not settled. Safe and worth sharing. Citations verified directly.
