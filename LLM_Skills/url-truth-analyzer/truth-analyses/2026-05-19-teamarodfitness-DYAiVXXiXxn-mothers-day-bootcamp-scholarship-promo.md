## Analysis

### SORT Analysis (Medical content)

**The post contains essentially no testable medical claims of its own.** It is a promotional / direct-response sales asset. The only assertions are:

1. *"Functional nutrition coaching with TAF will help you level up"* — vague, unfalsifiable marketing language; no specific health outcome named.
2. *Implicit framing that Hashimoto's moms have "put themselves on the back burner"* — emotional positioning, not a medical claim.
3. *Mother's Day scholarship = partial discount on enrollment fee* — a commercial offer, not a clinical recommendation.

There are no dosages, no protocols, no comparative effectiveness claims, no symptom-improvement statistics, no labs, and no citations.

#### Pass 1 — Four lenses

- **Safety**: Not addressed. No risks, harms, contraindications, or side effects of the bootcamp are mentioned. Programs marketed to autoimmune-thyroid populations routinely involve elimination diets (gluten, dairy, nightshades), high-dose supplementation, and direct-to-consumer functional lab panels — none of which are disclosed here, and none of which are risk-free in the Hashimoto's population (e.g., elimination diets can mask micronutrient deficiencies; functional lab ordering can drive interventions on subclinical or non-pathological findings).
- **Outcomes**: No outcomes are claimed in the 3 actual slides of this post. None are POEMs; none are DOEs; the post is a discount offer.
- **Risk of bias**: Maximum possible bias — the post is authored by the seller, the audience is a self-selected pool of prior sales-call leads, and the gating criterion ("finances were the ONLY reason you didn't move forward") is calibrated to convert near-buyers, not to identify the people most likely to benefit clinically.
- **Total evidence**: Functional-nutrition coaching as a treatment modality for Hashimoto's has no RCT-grade evidence supporting symptom or antibody-titre improvement beyond standard endocrinology care (levothyroxine, monitoring, lifestyle counseling). Specific interventions her program likely uses (AIP diet, selenium, low-dose naltrexone, gluten elimination in non-celiac patients) have inconsistent and at best Grade B / C evidence for thyroid antibody reduction without consistent POEM benefit.

#### Pass 2 — SORT Grade

**Grade C** — because the post itself makes no specific evidence-based claim, the implicit "our program helps Hashimoto's" claim is supported only by testimonial-style social proof (and the social proof in this carousel is, as the visual analysis below shows, partially borrowed from older unrelated posts). Even by the most charitable reading, this is anecdote + expert opinion of an unlicensed coach + commercial endorsement — the textbook definition of Grade C.

## Verdict
This is a **promotional asset, not a medical post**, and should be read as such. There are no testable health claims to validate, no evidence cited, and no safety information disclosed about the program being sold. The implicit pitch — that paid functional-nutrition coaching by an unlicensed practitioner improves Hashimoto's — has, at best, Grade C support in the broader literature, and this post does not even attempt to make that case in writing. By SORT standards: **Grade C**. As a consumer signal: a "Mother's Day scholarship" that is only available to prior strategy-call leads is a textbook conversion mechanism, not a charitable gift. If the underlying program were medically beneficial, the case for it would be made with clinical outcomes (TSH/T3/T4/TPO antibody trajectories, validated symptom scales, comparator arms), not with selfies and a discount code.
