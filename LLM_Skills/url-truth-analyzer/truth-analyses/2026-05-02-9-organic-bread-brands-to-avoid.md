## Analysis

### SORT Analysis

#### Safety
The video reports specific glyphosate levels in parts per billion (ppb) for each brand — this is useful quantitative data. However, it omits critical context: the EPA's daily exposure limit for glyphosate is 2 mg/kg body weight/day, and the detected ppb levels in bread are orders of magnitude below this threshold even with daily consumption. The video presents all detected glyphosate as inherently dangerous without discussing dose-response relationships. Relative risk framing ("6 times the level of Nature's Own") is used without absolute risk context.

#### Outcomes
The primary outcome reported is glyphosate detection levels — a Disease-Oriented Endpoint (DOE), not a patient-oriented outcome. Health claims about "gut microbiome disruption, liver inflammation, and neurological damage" are stated as facts but no human clinical trials are cited linking bread-level glyphosate exposure to these outcomes. The IARC "probable carcinogen" classification (Group 2A) for glyphosate is real but is based primarily on occupational exposure studies, not dietary exposure at ppb levels.

#### Risks of Bias
- Cites real organizations (Detox Project, Florida DOH, IARC) — adds credibility
- However, cherry-picks the IARC classification while omitting the EPA's conclusion that glyphosate is "not likely to be carcinogenic to humans" and EFSA's similar finding
- Moms Across America testing is cited — this organization has been criticized by scientists for methodology
- Countdown format with emotional language ("betrayal," "hiding," "danger zone") indicates engagement-driven framing
- Recommendations at the end (Ezekiel, One Degree) function as implicit endorsements
- No peer-reviewed human studies cited for health claims at dietary exposure levels

#### Total Evidence
Glyphosate contamination in food is a genuine, well-documented issue. The Detox Project and EWG have published legitimate testing data. However, the scientific consensus is divided: IARC classifies glyphosate as Group 2A (probable carcinogen) based largely on occupational/agricultural exposure, while the EPA, EFSA, and WHO/FAO Joint Meeting on Pesticide Residues concluded dietary exposure levels are safe. The video presents only one side of this scientific debate and extrapolates from detection to harm without clinical evidence.

#### SORT Grade: C

No RCTs or systematic reviews of health outcomes from dietary glyphosate exposure at the levels detected. Claims rest on observational associations, mechanistic reasoning ("glyphosate disrupts gut microbiome"), and extrapolation from occupational exposure studies to dietary exposure. The testing data is real, but the health conclusions drawn are not supported by clinical outcome evidence.

## Verdict

**Real data, overstated danger.** The glyphosate detection numbers are from legitimate testing sources and the concern is not fabricated. However, the video fails to contextualize these ppb-level findings against EPA and EFSA safety thresholds, which are far higher than what's found in bread. It cherry-picks the IARC classification while ignoring contradictory regulatory conclusions. The recommended alternatives (Ezekiel, One Degree) are genuinely cleaner products, but the fear framing around the "avoid" brands overstates proven risk to consumers.
