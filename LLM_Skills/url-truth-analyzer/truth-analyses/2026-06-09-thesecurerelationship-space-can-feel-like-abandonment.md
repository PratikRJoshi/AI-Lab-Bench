## Analysis

### Science: Claim Validation
This maps cleanly onto attachment research. Anxiously attached individuals are hypervigilant to cues of partner withdrawal and experience hyperactivation (escalating distress) when they perceive distance [1]. The "pursue-withdraw" dynamic — where one partner's need for space spikes the other's abandonment fear — is a documented, self-reinforcing pattern [1]. The nuance that the same behavior regulates one nervous system while alarming another is accurate and clinically useful. The advice ("how you step back matters") is reasonable and non-harmful. Caveat: "almost identical to abandonment" is a felt-experience description, not a claim that space *is* abandonment — readers shouldn't conclude they can never take space.

## ELI5 — Friend to Friend
If your partner gets anxious, you suddenly going quiet or pulling away can feel to them like you're leaving — even when you just need a breather. That's a real, studied reaction, not them being "dramatic." So take your space, but say something first like "I need 20 minutes, I'm not going anywhere." That tiny sentence prevents a lot of panic.
