## Analysis

### Medical: SORT Analysis

**Extracted claims**
1. TSH levels fluctuate throughout the day; a 2pm test can look "completely different" from an 8am one. Book between 8–10am and stick to the same time every test.
2. Coffee/tea (even black) and food before the draw can skew thyroid numbers because "caffeine affects absorption." Water only.
3. Taking your thyroid meds (levothyroxine) before the draw "artificially spikes your T4 levels and doesn't reflect your actual baseline." Skip the morning dose, do the draw, take meds straight after.

**Pass 1 — Four lenses**

- **Safety**: Low harm. Advice to skip a single morning levothyroxine dose until after the draw and then take it is standard endocrinology lab-prep guidance (American Thyroid Association patient info, NICE NG145 pre-analytic recommendations). Skipping one dose causes no clinical destabilisation because levothyroxine's half-life is ~7 days. She does not tell viewers to alter ongoing dose or stop the drug. Indirect harm signal: a strong "your results are wrong unless..." framing can feed test-anxiety and the broader "your doctor is missing it" narrative that drives patients into paid functional-medicine programs (her CTA slide).
- **Outcomes (POEM vs DOE)**: All three claims are about pre-analytic accuracy of lab markers (TSH, free T4) — these are **disease-oriented endpoints (DOEs)**, not patient-oriented outcomes (symptom relief, mortality, QoL). The supporting literature shows the tips improve measurement accuracy and avoid misclassification, but no RCT shows that following these rules changes patient-relevant outcomes such as symptom remission or quality of life. Per SORT, claims supported only by DOE evidence are capped at Grade B.
- **Risk of bias**: The clinical evidence for each claim comes from cohort/observational studies and RCT timing studies, not from a single influencer's opinion. The post itself cites no sources, but the underlying science is independently replicable. The post is monetised (funnel to paid program), but the lab-prep content itself is not a proprietary protocol.
- **Total evidence**: Claims align with mainstream endocrine guidance:
  - **Diurnal TSH variation**: TSH peaks ~2–4am and troughs in early-to-mid afternoon, with within-subject CoV ~31% over 24h. Late-morning non-fasting TSH averages ~26% lower than early-morning fasting TSH in the same patient on the same day, sufficient to reclassify ~6% of subclinical-hypothyroidism diagnoses. (Scobbo & VonDohlen 2004 / Bremner et al. 2012)
  - **Food intake**: Postmeal TSH is significantly lower than fasting TSH in the same patient; free T4 is relatively stable. (Mahwi & Abdulateef 2017)
  - **Coffee + levothyroxine**: Coffee impairs intestinal absorption of levothyroxine — clinically documented in patients taking the two together. This is most relevant for ongoing dosing, not specifically for blood-draw prep, so the on-slide framing slightly conflates "coffee affects absorption" with "coffee skews the test itself"; the cleaner mechanism is that food/coffee independently nudges TSH. (Benvenga et al. 2008)
  - **Levothyroxine before draw**: Free T4 peaks 2–4h post-dose; 72–81% of "high free T4 with non-suppressed TSH" results occur in levothyroxine users due to timing of dose relative to draw. ATA explicitly advises drawing labs *before* the morning dose. (Bornschein et al. 2023; Bach-Huynh et al. 2009)

**Pass 2 — Grade**: **Grade B**
All three tips are correct in direction and consistent with multiple independent studies and ATA/NICE guidance. They are capped at B (not A) because: (a) the endpoints are DOEs (lab-marker accuracy), not POEMs; (b) the relevant studies are cohort/cross-over timing studies, not large outcome RCTs; and (c) Claim 2 slightly conflates the mechanism of caffeine ↔ levothyroxine absorption with the mechanism of caffeine ↔ TSH measurement.

## Verdict
This is the cleanest medical-information post on Aggie McCabe's grid that we have reviewed in this batch. All three pre-analytic tips are correct and align with mainstream endocrinology guidance (ATA / NICE NG145 / standard endocrine lab protocols): draw TSH in the early morning, fast (water only) before the draw, and hold the morning levothyroxine dose until after phlebotomy if free T4 is being measured. The evidence is consistent across multiple independent studies (Grade B by SORT — capped from A because the endpoints are lab-marker accuracy, not patient-oriented outcomes like symptom remission). Caveat: the post is structurally a funnel — it ends with "Comment REALITY for my free training" pointing to her paid program — so while the standalone slides are reliable, viewers should take the embedded clinical advice and not feel obligated to follow the funnel. The minor on-slide error (caffeine "affects absorption" framing in Slide 3 slightly conflates the levothyroxine-coffee absorption mechanism with the food/coffee-TSH measurement mechanism) is stylistic, not consequential to the takeaway.
