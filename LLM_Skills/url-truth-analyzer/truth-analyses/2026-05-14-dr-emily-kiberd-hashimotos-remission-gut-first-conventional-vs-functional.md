## Analysis

### SORT Analysis

#### Claim 1: Comparison table — conventional advice vs "what worked"

| Conventional Advice | Her Counter-Claim | Evidence Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| "Take your thyroid medication" | Address what's making medication not work (gut infections, mold, nutrient deficiencies) | **Mixed** — nutrient cofactors (selenium, iron, vitamin D) are evidence-supported; mold is speculative |
| "Go gluten-free" | Identify what's driving inflammation (it's often not gluten) | **Misleading** — Hashimoto's patients have 4-5x celiac prevalence; gluten is MORE relevant here |
| "Eat less and move more" | Eat more protein, lift heavier, train less | **Partially supported** — resistance training benefits thyroid patients, but "train less" is vague |
| "Your labs look normal" | Run the right labs — TSH alone tells you almost nothing | **Legitimate** — Full thyroid panel (TSH + FT3 + FT4 + TPO + TgAb) is genuinely superior to TSH-only |
| "Reduce stress" | Identify what's triggering your cortisol (mold and infections drive stress responses) | **Speculative** — mold causing cortisol elevation is mechanistic speculation, not clinical evidence |
| "This is just how Hashimoto's is" | Hashimoto's can go into remission | **Contested** — "remission" (antibody reduction) occurs but is not equivalent to cure; antibodies fluctuate naturally |
| "Come back in 6 months" | Address root cause now before more thyroid tissue is destroyed | **Legitimate concern** — progressive thyroid destruction is real; early intervention is appropriate |

#### Claim 2: "Why Fixing Your Gut First Matters" infographic

Claims made:
- "Your gut controls 70% of your immune system" — **Supported** (GALT contains ~70% of immune cells; this is established immunology)
- "Gut inflammation directly triggers thyroid autoimmunity" — **Partially supported** (association demonstrated, causation not proven in humans)
- "Hashimoto's patients have significantly higher intestinal permeability" — **Supported** (PMID 22109896 — Sasso et al. do show increased permeability markers in Hashimoto's patients)
- "Low stomach acid is common in Hashimoto's patients" — **Supported** (hypochlorhydria prevalence ~30-40% in autoimmune thyroiditis; PMID 3133579)
- "Fix the gut first: Test with GI-MAP, Address infections, Restore stomach acid, Heal gut lining" — **Grade C** — While the problems identified have evidence, the specific protocol (GI-MAP testing → address → restore → heal) has no RCT evidence showing it improves Hashimoto's outcomes

#### PMID 22109896 verification:
This is Sasso FC et al., "Ultrasonographic and Biochemical Evidence of Intestinal Permeability in Patients with Autoimmune Thyroid Disease," 2012. **Real paper, relevant to claims.** However, it's a cross-sectional observational study showing association, not an intervention trial proving that "fixing" permeability improves thyroid outcomes.

## Verdict
This is the most substantive of the 8 posts, mixing legitimate medical observations (full thyroid panel, gut-thyroid association, nutrient cofactors) with unproven protocols (mold clearing, GI-MAP-guided gut repair as Hashimoto's treatment). The PMID citation adds credibility but is an observational study being used to justify interventional claims. The comparison table format creates a false binary between "useless conventional advice" and "my superior approach," when the reality is that both standard endocrinology AND some integrative approaches have evidence — and neither has RCT support for "putting Hashimoto's into remission."

## ELI5 — Friend to Friend
Mixed bag. She's actually right about two things: (1) a full thyroid panel is better than just TSH, and (2) there IS a gut-thyroid connection. But she wraps these legitimate points in unproven stuff (mold protocols, her specific program "puts Hashimoto's into remission") and uses it all to sell coaching. Take the "get more thorough labs" advice to a real endocrinologist. Skip the $500 masterclass.
