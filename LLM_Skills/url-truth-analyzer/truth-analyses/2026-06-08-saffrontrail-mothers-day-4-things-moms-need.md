## Analysis

### Science / EBM
The epidemiology and recommendations are accurate:
- **~Half of Indian women over 50 have osteoporosis/low bone density**: a systematic review estimates osteoporosis in 8–62% of postmenopausal Indian women; a low-income cohort (ages 30–60) found **52% osteopenia + 29% osteoporosis** [1][2]. "Roughly half have osteoporosis or low bone density" is well within (indeed conservative for) these figures. Indian women also show earlier onset and higher fracture prevalence than Western populations [2]. ✔
- **Sarcopenia starts in the 30s, accelerates after menopause**: age-related muscle loss begins around the 30s and accelerates with menopausal hormonal decline (estrogen, DHEA, GH/IGF-1); Indian sarcopenia prevalence runs ~10–43.6% [3][4]. ✔ Resistance training (plus protein and vitamin D) is the established intervention to slow/reverse it [3][4]. ✔
- **Most Indian women deficient in vitamin D and B12; iron deficiency common**: multiple micronutrient deficiencies (iron, B12, vitamin D) are "widely prevalent across age strata" in India; B12 deficiency ~16–62% in studies; anemia highly prevalent (NFHS-level data put anemia in well over half of Indian women) [4]. ✔ "Tired all the time = normalized iron deficiency" is an accurate clinical framing.
- **Loneliness raises dementia and stroke risk**: social isolation/loneliness is an established risk factor for dementia, stroke, and cardiovascular disease and increased mortality (consistent with the social-connection literature). ✔ (general, well-replicated)
- **Recommendations** — DEXA, vitamin D/B12/ferritin/thyroid/lipids/HbA1c screening, 2×/week strength training, adequate protein at every meal, 7 hours sleep, social connection — are all standard preventive-medicine advice for midlife/older women. ✔

No overreach; this is responsible, evidence-aligned public-health messaging.
