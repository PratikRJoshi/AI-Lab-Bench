#### Pass 1 — Four lenses

- **Safety**: The dominant safety risk of a "reverse Hashimoto's via lifestyle" message is that viewers with established overt hypothyroidism interpret it as license to discontinue or down-titrate levothyroxine. Untreated overt hypothyroidism — particularly in women of reproductive age, which is this creator's audience — is associated with infertility, miscarriage, preterm delivery, dyslipidemia, and, at the extreme, myxedema. The reel does not include a safety disclaimer in the visible frame.
- **Outcomes (POEM vs DOE)**: The strongest evidence in this space is on **disease-oriented** surrogates — anti-TPO/anti-Tg antibody titer reduction with selenium or curcumin, TSH normalization in subclinical cases — not on **patient-oriented** outcomes like sustained drug-free euthyroidism, normal fertility without thyroid hormone support, or absence of long-term cardiovascular sequelae. "Reversal" is a POEM the literature has not established at population level.
- **Risk of bias**: The claim is an n=1 personal testimonial, monetized via coaching programs. Selection bias, survivorship bias, and recall bias are unaddressed. No controls, no follow-up, no objective verification of pre-/post-treatment thyroid function or antibody titers is shown in the captured frame.
- **Total evidence**: Mainstream endocrine bodies do not recognize a curative lifestyle protocol for Hashimoto's. Adjunctive nutritional interventions (selenium ~200 µg/day, vitamin D repletion, gluten elimination in NCGS/celiac overlap, anti-inflammatory diet ± curcumin) have modest, mostly antibody-level evidence and are framed in the literature as **adjuncts to** — not **replacements for** — levothyroxine where indicated.

#### Pass 2 — SORT grade

- **Strong-form claim ("Hashimoto's reversed by 7 habits; implies replacement of standard care")**: **Grade C** — based on personal testimony, expert opinion (functional medicine), and disease-oriented evidence only. Not supported by ATA / Endocrine Society / NICE guidelines for overt hypothyroidism.
- **Weak-form claim ("Lifestyle habits can reduce TPO antibodies and inflammation as an adjunct")**: would rise to **Grade B** for specific interventions (selenium, vitamin D repletion in deficiency, gluten elimination in celiac/NCGS overlap), but this weaker framing is *not* what the on-screen overlay says.

The claim as presented in the thumbnail ("REVERSE MY HASHIMOTO'S") is therefore SORT **Grade C** and should not be shared as actionable medical advice. The seven habits cannot be evaluated individually because they are not in the captured data; if and when the actual list is recovered, each habit would need its own SORT pass (especially any habit involving stopping or reducing levothyroxine, doing a "mycotoxin/mold detox," or undertaking iodine-related interventions, all of which carry specific risk profiles).
