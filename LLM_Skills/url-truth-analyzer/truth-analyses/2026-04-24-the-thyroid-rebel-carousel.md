### EBM SORT Analysis

#### Pass 1: Four Lenses

**1. Safety**
- **Red flag:** Image 5 describes a patient who "halved her meds" with no mention of medical supervision, endocrinologist involvement, or monitoring. Reducing levothyroxine without guidance risks hypothyroid crisis, especially during pregnancy when thyroid hormone requirements *increase*.
- No absolute risks, contraindications, or side effects mentioned anywhere.
- No "consult your doctor" disclaimer visible in any image.
- The "root causes" framing implicitly positions medication as inadequate, which may discourage adherence.

**2. Outcomes**
- Claim 1 (trauma–thyroid link): Association claim. The evidence is observational (DOE at best — antibody levels, not patient outcomes).
- Claim 2 (exercise): Some RCTs show TSH reduction (DOE) and quality-of-life improvements (POEM), but evidence quality is low-to-moderate.
- Claim 4 (remission + halved meds): POEM claimed (feels better, got pregnant), but from a single anecdote — not a clinical trial. "Remission" is not a standardized clinical endpoint for Hashimoto's.
- Claim 6 (emotional root causes): Observational association only. No POEM data.
- Claim 7 (inner critic → symptoms): Mechanistic reasoning. One RCT on stress management showed antibody reduction (DOE), not symptom-oriented outcomes.
- **Maximum grade limited:** Most outcomes are DOEs or uncontrolled anecdotes.

**3. Risk of Bias**
- Zero citations in any image.
- The core evidence offered is a single patient testimonial (level 7 — anecdote).
- No mention of study designs, sample sizes, or comparison groups.
- No verifiable credentials for the content creator.
- Emotionally charged framing ("hopeful perspective," "rebel," "her words!!") appeals to affect, not evidence.
- Possible commercial motivation (coaching/programs), though not explicitly shown in these images.

**4. Total Evidence**
- **Trauma–thyroid:** A 2025 meta-analysis found a modest association (RR 1.291) between PTSD and autoimmune diseases broadly, but a 2024 Mendelian randomization study found *no significant causal relationship* specifically between PTSD and autoimmune thyroiditis. The carousel presents this as established fact.
- **Hashimoto's remission:** Small RCTs show lifestyle interventions (AIP diet, stress management, calorie restriction) can reduce thyroid antibody titers (DOE). However, antibody reduction ≠ clinical remission, and the AIP diet trial found *no significant change* in thyroid function or antibodies despite symptom improvement. "Remission" is not an established clinical outcome in Hashimoto's guidelines.
- **Exercise:** Generally supported as safe and possibly beneficial. Two systematic reviews/meta-analyses exist with conflicting conclusions on TSH impact but agreement on secondary health benefits. This is the best-supported claim in the carousel.
- **Emotional root causes:** One case-control study found higher rates of emotional neglect/abuse in women with autoimmune thyroid disorders vs. controls. Association, not causation. Calling these "root causes" is an overstatement.
- **Mainstream guideline alignment:** ATA/AACE guidelines do not recognize lifestyle modification alone as sufficient treatment for Hashimoto's. Levothyroxine remains standard of care.

### Overall SORT Grade: C

Consensus, expert opinion, anecdote, and disease-oriented evidence only. The carousel relies on a single patient testimonial as its strongest "proof," provides no citations, and makes claims that range from modestly supported (exercise) to unsupported (medication reduction, emotional root causes). The one claim meriting Grade B (exercise) is buried among Grade C claims. The overall content does not meet the threshold for Grade B.

## Verdict

**Grade C — Use with caution.** This carousel mixes a kernel of emerging science (trauma–autoimmunity association, exercise benefits) with oversimplified claims, anecdotal evidence, and potentially dangerous advice (reducing medication without stated medical supervision). The creator has no verified medical credentials. Patients should not use this content to make treatment decisions — particularly regarding medication changes — without consulting an endocrinologist.

**Key concerns:**
- The testimonial implying medication reduction is safe or desirable could cause direct patient harm, especially in pregnancy where thyroid hormone requirements increase.
- "Root causes" and "remission" language overstates what the current evidence supports.
- No citations, no disclaimers, no nuance about the limitations of the evidence.

## ELI5

This Instagram carousel says trauma and emotions can cause thyroid problems, and that you can put Hashimoto's disease into remission by changing your lifestyle — one woman even cut her medication in half. While there *is* some early research connecting stress to thyroid issues, and exercise is generally good for everyone including thyroid patients, the big claims here go way beyond what science has proven. Cutting your thyroid medication without a doctor is dangerous, especially if you're pregnant. The person making these posts doesn't appear to have medical training. Think of it like someone saying "I ate more vegetables and my cold went away" — maybe true for them, but that doesn't make it medical advice you should follow. **Talk to your doctor before changing anything about your thyroid treatment.**
